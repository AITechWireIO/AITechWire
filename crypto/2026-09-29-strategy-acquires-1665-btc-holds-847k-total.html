Strategy Acquires 1,665 BTC, Holds 847k Total

Michael Saylor's Strategy bought 1,665 Bitcoin and repurchased $152M STRC as of 9/27/26. This reinforces institutional BTC accumulation amid $84k prices and ETF inflows.

AI TechWire · September 29, 2026 · 5 min read
Photo by Jonathan Borba / Unsplash

Michael Saylor doesn't do subtle. While the broader market spent last week wrestling with oil-driven volatility and a gold selloff, Strategy's Executive Chairman posted two words on Sunday — "Even more orange" — and then proceeded to back them up with $142.7 million worth of Bitcoin. That's the playbook, and it hasn't changed.

The Purchase: 1,665 BTC, Second Week Running

According to a Monday 8-K filing, Strategy (Nasdaq: MSTR) acquired 1,665 Bitcoin between September 21 and September 27, paying an average of $85,681 per coin for a total outlay of $142.7 million. The purchase brings the company's cumulative holdings to 847,666 BTC, acquired for $63.95 billion at an average price of $75,437 per coin — a position now valued at approximately $70.3 billion.

This marks Strategy's second consecutive weekly purchase. The week prior, the company had acquired 950 BTC for $75.7 million, ending what had been a stretch of skipped weeks. Back-to-back buys totaling 2,615 BTC for $218.4 million in just a fortnight signal that Strategy's accumulation engine is back in full gear.

Critically, Strategy now holds more than 4% of Bitcoin's total supply — more than any other publicly traded company on the planet. That figure is not a rounding error. It is a structural market fact that every portfolio manager touching BTC exposure needs to internalize.

"Even more orange"

- Michael Saylor, Executive Chairman of Strategy, via social media post preceding the Monday purchase announcement

How the Deal Was Funded — And Why It Matters

Unlike the prior week's purchase, which was drawn from Strategy's USD reserve, this acquisition was financed through equity. The company sold approximately 1.47 million MSTR shares through its at-the-market program, generating $246.2 million in net proceeds. Those proceeds were then split with surgical precision:

  • $142.7 million — Bitcoin purchase
  • $103.5 million — STRC preferred share buybacks

To complete the STRC repurchase program, Strategy bought back approximately 1.53 million STRC shares for a combined $151.7 million — with the remaining $48.1 million sourced from its cash balance. The company also allocated $22.1 million from its USD reserve toward preferred dividends.

As of September 27, Strategy held over $5 billion in its USD reserve and $1 billion in USD cash. The company reported $723.5 million remaining for preferred repurchases and $1 billion authorized for MSTR buybacks. Notably, Strategy reported no sales of STRF, STRC, STRK, or STRD through its ATM programs during the period — a clean balance sheet signal.

The shift back to stock-sale funding is a meaningful tell. It suggests management is comfortable diluting equity at current MSTR prices to acquire Bitcoin — a calculated bet that BTC appreciation will outpace any dilutive drag. Given MSTR closed Friday at $158.61, having recently reclaimed the 200-day EMA at $152.68 and broken a descending trendline from May's highs, the market appears to be giving that thesis the benefit of the doubt — for now.

The ETF Backdrop: Institutions Are Moving

Strategy isn't buying into a vacuum. The same week its purchase was confirmed, spot Bitcoin ETFs posted their best week in nearly a year, pulling in $2.4 billion in net inflows for the period ending September 25 — a haul not seen since early October 2025.

The breakdown of where the capital landed is telling. BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) absorbed approximately $1.2 billion of the week's total. Fidelity's FBTC came in second with roughly $702 million. ARK 21Shares' ARKB rounded out the top three at approximately $295 million. Combined, those three products captured more than 90% of the week's institutional flows.

Year-to-date net flows for spot Bitcoin ETFs now sit at approximately positive $934 million — a dramatic reversal from a deficit of roughly $5.8 billion as recently as mid-July. Since their January 2024 launch, spot Bitcoin ETFs have accumulated cumulative net inflows exceeding $57 billion, with total assets under management reaching approximately $108 billion by late September 2026.

Source: CryptoBriefing - "US spot Bitcoin ETFs just had their best week in nearly a year. The products collectively pulled in $2.4 billion in net inflows for the week ending September 25, a haul not seen since early October 2025." link

Monday alone saw inflows peak at nearly $999 million — a figure that would have been a standout weekly total just months ago. Momentum did cool as the week progressed, with Friday's inflow tapering to $135 million, coinciding with Bitcoin retreating from a brief touch of $87,000 to the $83,000–$84,000 range.

The Bigger Picture: Demand Is Stacking

Here's what the combined data tells you: two of the most consequential Bitcoin demand channels — corporate treasury accumulation and spot ETF inflows — fired simultaneously last week. Strategy's mechanical, conviction-driven buying pairs with institutional ETF allocation in a way that creates layered, structural demand rather than the speculative froth that characterized earlier BTC cycles.

Saylor's "Even more orange" post may read as social media theater, but the $142.7 million 8-K filed the following Monday is a legal document. When a company holds more than 4% of a fixed-supply asset and continues buying through equity dilution — with $5 billion-plus still sitting in reserve — that's not a trade. That's a thesis executed at scale.

The ETF channel absorbed $2.4 billion in a single week. Strategy added 1,665 coins. Bitcoin sat in the low-to-mid $80,000 range. If both demand streams sustain anything close to this pace into Q4, the price pressure mathematics become difficult to ignore — and so does the compounding position of the world's largest corporate Bitcoin holder.

MSTR bulls have a key resistance level to clear at $169.52, the recent swing high. The SAR at $133.46 remains well below current price, keeping the uptrend intact. Whether the stock can break higher will depend heavily on where Bitcoin goes — and right now, the demand side of that ledger is looking increasingly crowded with buyers.
