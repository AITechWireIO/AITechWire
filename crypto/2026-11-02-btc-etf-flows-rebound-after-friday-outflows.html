BTC ETF Flows Rebound After Friday Outflows

ETH ETFs extended inflows streak despite weekly net outflows.

AI TechWire · November 02, 2026 · 4 min read
Photo by Yiğit Ali Atasoy / Unsplash

Binance weathered $3.64 billion in net outflows after reopening withdrawals Sunday — a bruising number that nevertheless tells a more resilient story than the headline suggests. The world's largest crypto exchange is fighting to hold user trust after a record $6.3 billion hack, and the early data suggests it is, for now, winning that fight.

What Happened: The Floodgates Open

Between 6 a.m. and 3:30 p.m. UTC on November 1, Binance processed $4.65 billion in withdrawals against $1.01 billion in deposits — a net outflow of $3.64 billion in the first nine and a half hours after withdrawal services were restored. The exchange had suspended withdrawals following a $6.3 billion security breach, the largest in crypto exchange history, attributed by the FBI to North Korean hackers operating under the name TraderTraitor.

The numbers are large in isolation. In context, they are considerably less alarming. Binance's latest proof-of-reserves data placed total assets at $171 billion. The $3.64 billion outflow represents 2.1% of that figure — meaningful, but not existential. The exchange itself noted that withdrawal volumes declined and began stabilizing after an initial spike, while deposit volumes "remained substantial."

Restoration of services was phased. By 12:36 p.m. UTC on November 1, all 15 networks and assets in the initial phase were back online — including Bitcoin, Ethereum, BNB, Solana, XRP, Dogecoin, and key stablecoins USDT and USDC. Testing for remaining networks and assets was continuing, with the exchange saying restoration priority would be based on market capitalization, locked funds, and user base size.

Source: Binance via X — "Circle activated its incident response procedures and coordinated with law enforcement to freeze more than $1.1 billion in USDC associated with the incident." link

Circle, the stablecoin issuer behind USDC, confirmed to CNBC that it had frozen over $1.1 billion in USDC linked to the hack — an update from a Friday statement by Circle CEO Jeremy Allaire, who said at that point more than $800 million had been frozen. The investigation, Circle said, remains ongoing.

Why It Matters: Recovery Math and SAFU

Binance's public commitment from the outset was blunt: it would absorb the financial impact of the stolen funds rather than pass losses to users. As of its latest update, the exchange said it had covered approximately 95% of the impacted amount, up from 90% in a previous update. The remaining 5% consists of less liquid tokens that will take longer to source in the required quantities.

The exchange confirmed that affected customer funds would be fully compensated using internal reserves and the Secure Asset Fund for Users, known as SAFU — Binance's long-standing insurance reserve designed precisely for scenarios like this.

Co-CEO Yi He addressed the sequencing of withdrawal restoration, noting that remaining cryptocurrency wallets were still undergoing security audits and that prioritization would be guided by market cap, locked funds, and the scale of the affected user base. The dual co-CEO structure — Richard Teng and Yi He — has meant two public voices managing the crisis communications simultaneously, a dynamic that is either reassuring in its depth or noisy in its coordination, depending on your vantage point.

"Trust isn't earned by what you say in good times. It's earned by what you do in hard times."

- Changpeng Zhao, Binance founder and former CEO

Changpeng Zhao, Binance's founder who stepped down as CEO in 2023 and was pardoned by President Trump, took to X to offer reassurance. His claim: "98% choose to stay!" — referring to the share of users who did not withdraw funds in the immediate aftermath of withdrawal restoration. That figure is striking, though it is worth noting it reflects behavior in the first nine-plus hours, not a sustained trend.

The Strongest Counter-View: 2.1% Is Not Zero

The 2.1% framing is Binance's framing. Critics will — rightly — note that $3.64 billion in net outflows within a single morning is an unprecedented stress test that no exchange would voluntarily choose. The $6.3 billion hack was itself a record. Circle's USDC freeze now exceeds $1.1 billion, and the investigation is still open. The FBI's attribution to North Korea's TraderTraitor operation adds a geopolitical dimension that does not resolve with a single proof-of-reserves report.

The open question is whether the stabilization in withdrawal volumes that Binance reported late Sunday holds through the week, or whether a second wave of departures materializes once users who were slower to react — or who faced network congestion — complete their exits. Binance has not provided a timeline for restoring withdrawals across all remaining cryptocurrency networks. Until that full restoration is complete and audited, the exchange remains operationally constrained.

There is also a regulatory dimension building in the background. The 2026 US midterm elections, held November 3, will determine which party controls Congress for the remainder of President Trump's second term. A Democratic takeover of either chamber could shift the legislative environment for crypto regulation, potentially introducing friction for exchanges operating in or adjacent to US markets. Democrats are currently leading in five of seven key Senate battleground states, according to New York Times/Siena University polling.

Resolution: A Controlled Bleed, For Now

The early verdict on Binance's recovery is cautiously constructive. A $3.64 billion outflow sounds alarming until you place it against a $171 billion asset base and a stabilizing withdrawal curve. The 95% recovery of stolen funds, the SAFU backstop, and the phased, orderly restoration of services all point to an exchange that had a genuine contingency plan — and is executing it.

What Binance cannot control is the reputational long tail. The hack will be studied, cited, and used as regulatory leverage for months. Whether 98% of users truly stayed, as Zhao claims, or whether a slower drift accelerates in the days ahead, will be the real test of whether Sunday's outflow was a contained event or the opening chapter of a larger exodus.

What It Means

  • Binance's immediate crisis is stabilizing, but the 5% of stolen funds still unrecovered — consisting of illiquid tokens — represents a lingering liability that could widen if market conditions deteriorate.
  • Circle's expanding USDC freeze (now past $1.1 billion, up from $800 million in two days) signals the investigation is actively widening, not narrowing — watch for further freezes or law enforcement actions tied to the TraderTraitor operation.
  • Binance's co-CEO structure is under its first major public stress test; how Teng and Yi He coordinate messaging over the coming days will shape institutional confidence in the exchange's governance.
  • The midterm election outcome on November 3 could reshape the US regulatory landscape for crypto exchanges — a Democratic Congress gaining committee power would likely mean a more aggressive oversight posture, adding external pressure on an exchange already managing an internal crisis.
