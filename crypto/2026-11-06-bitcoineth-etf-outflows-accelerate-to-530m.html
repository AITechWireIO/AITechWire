Bitcoin/ETH ETF Outflows Accelerate to $530M

Spot products see heavy redemptions Nov 5 as crypto tracks tech weakness. IBIT and ETHA down sharply alongside coin prices.

AI TechWire · November 06, 2026 · 4 min read
Photo by Kanchanara / Unsplash

Half a Billion Dollars Leaves Crypto ETFs in a Day — and the Exit Looks Deliberate

Wednesday's election-fueled euphoria lasted less than 24 hours. By Thursday's close on November 5, U.S. spot Bitcoin and Ether ETFs had shed a combined $529.97 million in a single session — erasing the prior day's $128 million in inflows and then some. This was not a glitch in the data or a one-fund anomaly. It was a coordinated retreat across the two largest crypto asset classes, and it carries a signal that institutional investors would rather not send twice.

What Happened: A $530M Reversal in One Session

The numbers are clean and consequential. Spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $430 million in net outflows on November 5. Spot Ether ETFs added another $99.97 million. Together, the combined withdrawal of $529.97 million arrived just one day after Bitcoin had briefly climbed above $80,000 on the back of Democrats' victories in the U.S. midterm elections.

Fidelity's Wise Origin Bitcoin Fund (FBTC) led the selloff, posting approximately $200 million in outflows — roughly 46.5% of all Bitcoin ETF redemptions for the session. That figure is striking on its own. What makes it more telling is the reversal it represents: FBTC had attracted around $237 million in inflows on Wednesday alone. In less than two trading sessions, one of the largest spot Bitcoin ETFs in the market swung nearly $437 million in net flow direction.

BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) recorded $105 million in net outflows, extending what is now a five-consecutive-session outflow streak. IBIT has seen more than $1 billion withdrawn. Bitcoin ETFs' year-to-date net outflows stood at approximately $510 million following Thursday's close — a figure that reframes the narrative of 2026 as a year of steady institutional adoption.

On the Ether side, BlackRock's iShares Ethereum Trust ETF (ETHA) accounted for $75.20 million of the $99.97 million in total Ether ETF outflows — approximately 75.2% of the category's single-day redemptions concentrated in one product.

"I expect Nasdaq to bottom tomorrow. The same for Bitcoin, potentially after sweeping $75,000."

- Crypto analyst Michaël van de Poppe, via X, November 6, 2026

Bitcoin fell back below $76,000 on Thursday as the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite plunged 5.5% — its biggest single-day decline since April 2025, according to Reuters. The correlation was hard to miss: when tech sells off at that magnitude, crypto follows, and ETF holders move first.

Why It Matters: Institutional Caution Is the Story

Spot ETF flows are the cleanest proxy available for institutional demand. When retail sentiment shifts, it shows up in coin prices. When institutional sentiment shifts, it shows up here — in redemption queues, authorized participant activity, and basis trades unwinding. Thursday's data points to the latter.

The post-election rally had a specific logic: a Democratic midterm sweep was read by some market participants as a signal toward crypto-friendly regulatory posture, or at minimum, a reduced threat of hostile legislation. That thesis priced in fast and unwound faster. Markets often price anticipated policy shifts well before they are confirmed, which can trigger profit-taking once initial enthusiasm fades. November 5 was that fade, compressed into a single session.

The IBIT outflow streak is particularly worth watching. Five consecutive sessions of net outflows from the largest spot Bitcoin ETF on the market — with more than $1 billion pulled — suggests this is not isolated portfolio rebalancing. It reflects a sustained repositioning by the class of investors who use IBIT as their primary crypto exposure vehicle. These are not traders flipping in and out of meme coins. When they exit at scale, liquidity conditions downstream — in DeFi protocols, in miner treasury management, in derivatives markets — feel the pressure.

The Counter-View: One Day Is Not a Trend

The honest counter-argument deserves its moment. ETF redemptions do not automatically translate into equivalent selling on public exchanges. Fund operations, authorized participant mechanics, and in-kind creation/redemption baskets mean the relationship between outflow numbers and spot price impact is rarely one-to-one. Some portion of Thursday's withdrawals likely reflected profit-taking after a genuine two-day rally, not a structural shift in long-term conviction.

Dean Lyulkin, CEO of Cardiff and founder of investment publication The Dean's List, offered a macro reframe that matters here. He argued the current environment differs meaningfully from the period preceding the 2008 financial crisis, noting that household balance sheets and the banking system are considerably stronger today than they were ahead of that collapse. His point: a bad week in crypto ETF flows, coinciding with a sharp Nasdaq drawdown, does not automatically signal systemic unraveling.

Source: Dean Lyulkin, CEO of Cardiff - "The global financial crisis wasn't caused by Democrats taking Congress." link

That framing is worth holding. The open question is whether outflows persist into next week or stabilize as the election catalyst fully fades from the market's attention and macro conditions stabilize. One day of heavy redemptions, even at $530 million, does not close that question.

Resolution: The Rally Borrowed Time It Didn't Have

The post-midterm Bitcoin move above $80,000 was real — and it was borrowed. It borrowed from optimism about policy, from short-term momentum, and from a market that had already spent months absorbing regulatory uncertainty. When the Nasdaq dropped 5.5% in a single session, the loan was called in. The $529.97 million that left crypto ETFs on November 5 was the repayment.

What comes next depends on two things the sources make clear: whether IBIT's outflow streak breaks in the coming sessions, and whether Bitcoin finds a floor — van de Poppe's $75,000 level is the one traders are watching. If that level holds and ETF inflows resume, Thursday reads as a sharp but contained correction. If outflows continue at this pace, the year-to-date ETF flow picture darkens considerably from an already negative $510 million baseline.

What It Means

  • IBIT holders and BlackRock's ETF franchise face reputational pressure if the five-session outflow streak extends further — over $1 billion in withdrawals is a headline no product manager wants repeated.
  • Fidelity's FBTC swung nearly $437 million in net flow direction across two sessions, confirming it remains one of the most reactive products to short-term sentiment — a double-edged position in a volatile market.
  • Bitcoin's $75,000 level is the near-term technical line analysts are watching; a sustained break below it this likely accelerates further ETF redemptions and pressures miner revenue assumptions built on higher price floors.
  • Investors monitoring DeFi and miner balance sheets should treat sustained ETF outflows as a leading indicator — not a lagging one — for downstream liquidity stress across the broader crypto ecosystem.
