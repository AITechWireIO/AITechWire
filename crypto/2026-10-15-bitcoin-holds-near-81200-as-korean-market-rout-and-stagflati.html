Bitcoin Holds Its Ground as Korean Market Rout and Stagflation Fears Shake Risk Assets

Bitcoin is playing defense — and doing it better than almost everything else in the risk universe. While South Korea's Kospi plunged 8% and triggered circuit breakers, copper fell, Nasdaq futures slid, and altcoins buckled, Bitcoin dipped briefly toward $80,000 before clawing back to around $81,200. That relative composure is not a coincidence — it is a signal worth reading carefully.

What Happened: A Broad-Based Risk-Off Wave, Not a Crypto Story

Thursday's early-session move looked, at first glance, like another crypto sell-off. Bitcoin fell to just above $80,000 at around 02:30 UTC — near the October 8 low of roughly $80,300 — before recovering. Buyers stepped in, trimming the 1% loss to around 0.5% by the time European desks opened. But the pressure was never really about Bitcoin.

Asian equities set the macro tone. South Korea's Kospi closed 7.5% lower, taking week-to-date losses to 15.5%, with tech heavyweights bearing the brunt: SK Hynix fell 11% and Samsung Electronics finished 9.5% lower. Korea Exchange activated circuit breakers as the index closed at 5,554 points, its lowest daily close since April 8. Nasdaq 100 index futures slid about 1%, extending Wednesday's 1.9% drop in the Nasdaq Composite. Copper futures fell nearly 1%, compounding Wednesday's 1.6% decline. The message across asset classes was identical: get out of anything growth-sensitive.

The catalyst behind Wednesday's initial move — a hotter-than-expected U.S. CPI print showing September inflation at 3.7% year-on-year — injected hawkish anxiety into markets. The odds of an October Federal Reserve rate hike spiked to nearly 47% on CME's FedWatch before fading back to roughly 26%, with the remaining probability priced for rates to stay unchanged at 3.75%–4%. The quick reversal in rate-hike odds suggests the market is more worried about growth cracking than inflation persisting. That distinction matters.

Why It Matters: Stagflation Is the Macro Story Now

The constellation of moves — falling yields, rising gold, weak equities, soft copper — points toward a specific and uncomfortable macro narrative. The 10-year Treasury yield dropped eight basis points to 5.08%, extending its pullback from a recent peak of 5.36%. Gold climbed 1%. These are not the footprints of a simple risk-off trade driven by rate fears. They describe something more corrosive: stagflation.

"It feels more like a stagflationary environment where risk assets and the US dollar generally underperform, while gold outperforms."

- Giuseppe Dellamotta, InvestingLive analyst
Source: Giuseppe Dellamotta, InvestingLive - "It feels more like a stagflationary environment where risk assets and the US dollar generally underperform, while gold outperforms." CoinDesk Daybook

In a stagflationary regime, the Fed is trapped: inflation is too high to cut, but growth is deteriorating too fast to hike aggressively without triggering a credit crisis. QCP Capital's framing captured the dilemma precisely: "Does the beat force further hiking — or does credit stress prompt policy recalibration?" There is no clean answer, and that ambiguity is the market's actual problem.

For crypto, the downstream consequences showed up in the derivatives data. Nearly $1 billion in crypto futures positions were liquidated over 24 hours following the CPI print, up from roughly $136 million the day before. Ether longs took the hardest hit — $200 million liquidated versus $115 million in Bitcoin — pointing to heavier leverage concentration in ETH heading into the print. Trading volumes surged 61% to $252 billion in 24-hour futures turnover, but open interest held largely flat at $139.6 billion, meaning the activity was mostly existing positions being closed or churned rather than fresh leverage being built.

Bitcoin's Relative Resilience: Partial Haven or Falling Tide?

The divergence between Bitcoin and the rest of crypto is the most analytically interesting element of Thursday's session. The CoinDesk 80 — the broader altcoin basket — fell 2.2%. The bitcoin-dominant CoinDesk 5 lost just 0.7%. Ether dropped 2.0%. The CoinDesk Memecoin Index lost 4.0%, with pump.fun (PUMP) falling 9.4%. All but six members of the CoinDesk 100 finished lower.

That spread reinforces a pattern that has been building for months: in genuine macro stress events, Bitcoin decouples upward relative to altcoins. It does not behave like gold — it still falls — but it falls less, and it recovers faster. Analysts at 10x Research made the case explicit, saying they still prefer holding Bitcoin through the current AI trade unwind, but only as long as BTC holds above $78,000. Below that level, the calculus changes. They also recommend using Ether as a hedge for Bitcoin long positions — an acknowledgment that ETH's higher volatility can be monetized rather than simply endured.

The counter-argument is straightforward: Bitcoin's resilience is a feature of early stress, not prolonged stress. In a sustained stagflationary regime where risk assets underperform for quarters rather than sessions, BTC's partial haven status is unlikely to fully insulate it. Gold and Treasuries absorb that kind of persistent capital rotation. Bitcoin absorbs some of it at the margin. The open question is whether Thursday marks an escalation into that sustained regime or a sharp but containable shock.

The near-term data will sharpen that picture. Thursday's producer price index offers the next inflation read. A hot number adds fuel to the risk-off mood and could reignite hike pricing. SK Hynix and Samsung both report third-quarter earnings on October 28 and 29, respectively — and Samsung is expected to confirm Q3 sales of 195 trillion won with operating profit of roughly 107 trillion won. If those numbers disappoint, another leg of Korean equity weakness — and another round of crypto contagion — becomes a real scenario.

What It Means

  • Bitcoin's partial haven status is real but conditional: The CoinDesk 5's 0.7% loss versus the CoinDesk 80's 2.2% drop confirms BTC outperforms altcoins in stress — but the $78,000 level cited by 10x Research is the line that separates resilience from breakdown, and traders should treat it as the session's most important technical anchor.
  • Gold and Treasury yields are now the leading indicators for crypto: In a stagflationary macro setup, risk assets including Bitcoin follow gold and yields, not the other way around. Watch the 10-year yield's relationship to 5.08% and gold's ability to hold its 1% gain as the primary signals heading into the U.S. open.
  • Altcoin longs remain the most exposed position: With $200 million in ETH longs liquidated against $115 million in BTC, the leverage structure of the altcoin market is still unwinding — the 61% surge in futures volume with flat open interest confirms churning, not stabilization.
  • Thursday's PPI print and Korean earnings season will determine whether this is a shock or a regime: A hot PPI number likely extends the risk-off move and pressures Bitcoin back toward the $78,000–$80,000 zone; a soft print gives the partial haven thesis room to run.
