Metaplanet Denies $320M Bitcoin Sale Rumors

Metaplanet CEO dismissed reports of selling 43,000 BTC, affirming holdings amid corporate treasury strategies. Clarifies position as BTC price hovers near key levels.

AI TechWire · August 13, 2026 · 5 min read
Photo by Maxim Hopman / Unsplash
```html

Tokyo-listed Metaplanet is sitting on a $1.5 billion paper loss on its 43,000 BTC — and yet the sell rumors are apparently overblown. Here's what the numbers actually say.

The Rumor That Rocked the Market

In crypto, a well-placed rumor can do more damage than a genuine selloff. This week, reports began circulating that Metaplanet — the Tokyo-listed firm that has positioned itself as Asia's answer to Strategy — had quietly unloaded its entire bitcoin treasury, a stash of some 43,000 BTC. The implied scale: hundreds of millions of dollars in selling pressure hitting a market already navigating choppy price action near key technical levels.

Metaplanet's CEO moved quickly to deny the reports, reaffirming the company's holdings and doubling down on its corporate bitcoin treasury strategy. The denial matters — not just for Metaplanet shareholders, but for the entire corporate BTC adoption thesis that has been building momentum across institutional circles.

But the denial alone doesn't erase the uncomfortable reality underneath it. Because as of end-June, Metaplanet was carrying a paper loss of $1.5 billion on that very same 43,000 BTC position. That's not a rounding error. That's a balance sheet crater that demands scrutiny.

Paper Losses That Rival Entire Crypto Markets

To understand why the sell rumors gained traction in the first place, you have to look at what the concentration risk actually looks like across the corporate treasury landscape. Metaplanet isn't alone in its pain.

Strategy (MSTR), the world's largest public digital asset treasury company, reported a comparable paper loss of $8.2 billion last month. Add Metaplanet's $1.5 billion to that figure, and you're looking at nearly $10 billion in combined unrealized losses sitting on the books of just two publicly listed bitcoin treasury firms.

"The two largest publicly listed bitcoin treasury firms alone are sitting on paper losses big enough to rival the market value of major cryptocurrencies with strong use cases."

- CoinDesk Daybook, August 13, 2026

Bitcoin was hovering at $63,412.55 at the time of reporting — a price level that puts both firms in the red relative to their higher-cost acquisition strategies. The math is brutal and public. It's no wonder whispers of a liquidation event found an audience.

Source: CoinDesk / Omkar Godbole - "Early Thursday, Tokyo-listed Metaplanet (3350) revealed a paper loss of $1.5 billion on its 43,000 BTC as of end-June. Last month, Strategy (MSTR), the world's largest public digital asset treasury (DAT) company, reported a comparable paper loss of $8.2 billion." link

Why the Denial Matters More Than the Rumor

Corporate treasury strategies built around a single asset class live and die on conviction signaling. The moment a major holder blinks — or is perceived to blink — it creates a cascading narrative that invites short sellers, spooks retail investors, and puts downward pressure on the underlying asset. That's the game being played here, whether the rumors were deliberately seeded or organically mutated from misread filings.

Metaplanet's CEO pushing back publicly and forcefully is the only rational play. The company has staked its entire market identity on being a bitcoin treasury vehicle. A confirmed sale at current prices — booking a loss of that magnitude — would have been an existential reputational event, not just a portfolio adjustment.

More importantly, the denial supports a broader thesis that institutional and corporate BTC adoption is structural, not speculative. These firms aren't day trading. They are making multi-year, high-conviction bets that bitcoin's long-term value will vindicate short-term paper losses. That argument remains intact as long as the treasuries stay intact.

Still, the pressure is real. With BTC near $63,412 and $10 billion in combined unrealized losses staring down Strategy and Metaplanet's balance sheets simultaneously, the market is watching every disclosure, every executive statement, every SEC filing with microscopic attention.

The Concentration Risk Nobody Wants to Talk About

Here's the inconvenient editorial we need to run alongside the denial story: the concentration risk is extraordinary. Metaplanet's entire bet is on one token. Strategy's entire identity is built on one token. When that token moves against you, there is no hedge, no diversification, no fallback position. The losses compound in the open, in real time, in front of shareholders and regulators.

The combined nearly $10 billion in paper losses across these two firms alone should prompt serious questions from institutional allocators about what happens if bitcoin revisits lower levels. At what price does a board override a CEO's conviction? At what point do fiduciary duties collide with corporate identity?

For now, Metaplanet's CEO has drawn the line clearly: the 43,000 BTC stays. The corporate BTC adoption thesis gets another day in the sun. And the market, for the moment, has absorbed the rumor and the rebuttal without catastrophic disruption.

But $1.5 billion in paper losses has a way of making future denials sound a little less convincing each time they're needed. The bulls better hope bitcoin finds its footing — and fast.
```
