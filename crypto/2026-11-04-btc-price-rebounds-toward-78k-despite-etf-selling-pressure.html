BTC Price Rebounds Toward $78K Despite ETF Selling Pressure

AI TechWire · November 04, 2026 · 4 min read
Photo by Kanchanara / Unsplash

Bitcoin Fights Back: ETF Redemptions Test the Floor, But BTC Won't Stay Down

Bitcoin is doing what hodlers always said it would: absorbing institutional selling pressure and bouncing anyway. After bleeding more than half a billion dollars in ETF outflows through the first two trading sessions of November, BTC recovered toward $78,230 by early November 4 — a data point that matters more than the outflow headlines suggest.

What Happened: A Wave of Redemptions, Then a Rebound

The numbers were ugly on the surface. US-listed spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $115.6 million in net outflows on November 3, according to data from Farside Investors. That followed a much larger $398.6 million outflow on Monday, bringing total net outflows for the first two trading sessions of November to approximately $514.2 million. And it came on the heels of an already brutal end to October, when spot Bitcoin ETFs shed $643.2 million on October 30 alone.

BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) led the retreat on November 3, recording $95 million in net outflows. Grayscale's Bitcoin Mini Trust (BTC) followed with $52.6 million in withdrawals. The selling was concentrated, not systemic — several other funds reported little or no activity.

But the other side of the ledger tells a different story. Fidelity's FBTC attracted $6.8 million in fresh capital. Bitwise's BITB pulled in $21.9 million in inflows. Grayscale's GBTC added $3.3 million. Those inflows weren't enough to flip the overall daily figure positive, but they signal that not every institutional player is heading for the exits.

Meanwhile, Bitcoin's price refused to cooperate with the bearish narrative. BTC traded around $76,300 on November 3, fell as low as $75,600 overnight, then clawed its way back to $78,230 by early November 4. The divergence between the ETF flow data and the price action is the story.

"The divergence between Bitcoin's recovery and ETF outflows suggests that buying activity elsewhere in the market may be helping absorb institutional selling pressure."

- CoinPaper analysis, November 4, 2026

Why It Matters: The Bid Beneath the Market

When an asset absorbs more than half a billion dollars in net ETF redemptions over two sessions and still posts a recovery, something is buying. That's not noise — it's a structural signal. The question is what that bid represents and whether it holds.

The technical picture adds texture. Per Blockchain.news analysis of Farside Investors data, BTC was trading at $77,277 on the four-hour chart inside a bearish structure beneath the EMA50 at $78,442 and EMA200 at $80,812. The MACD was showing a golden cross, while RSI sat at a neutral 45.43 — not oversold, not euphoric. Upper Bollinger resistance sits near $78,704, with lower support at $76,432. The setup reads as a coiled spring: directionally undecided, but with momentum indicators leaning toward a test of resistance rather than a breakdown.

The $80,000 level is now the line that separates a consolidation from a resumption of the broader bull trend. Bitcoin is still below it. That matters. But the ability to recover from $75,600 to $78,230 without a catalyst — during continued ETF selling — is exactly the kind of price behavior that strengthens the case that the downside is limited.

The Counter-View: Institutional Caution Hasn't Cleared

The bear case is real and shouldn't be dismissed with a hand wave. Tuesday's $115.6 million outflow was smaller than the prior two sessions, but it was still an outflow — the third consecutive day of net redemptions from US spot Bitcoin ETFs. When the largest product in the market, BlackRock's IBIT, accounts for $95 million in a single day's withdrawals, that's not a retail panic — that's professional money making a deliberate decision to reduce exposure.

The November 6 US employment report is the next macro tripwire. Strong jobs data could push Federal Reserve rate expectations higher, tightening financial conditions and introducing fresh volatility into risk assets including crypto. That's the scenario where ETF outflows accelerate rather than moderate, and where BTC's tentative recovery gets unwound. The open question is whether the non-ETF bid — whatever it is — holds up against a macro headwind of that magnitude.

The pattern from late October is a warning: spot Bitcoin ETFs lost $643.2 million on a single day before this November wave began. Momentum can shift faster than the technicals suggest.

Resolution: The Thesis Holds, But $80K Is the Proof Point

What the data shows is a market with competing forces at work, and Bitcoin holding its own. ETF redemptions are real, concentrated, and not over. The buying that's absorbing them is real but unidentified — which makes it harder to trust. The recovery from $75,600 to $78,230 is meaningful, but it means less if BTC can't reclaim $80,000.

That level — the EMA200 sits at $80,812 — is where the structural case gets resolved. A clean reclaim with volume would validate the thesis that ETF selling is a temporary flush rather than a trend change. Failure to reach it, especially if the November 6 jobs report comes in hot, keeps the bears in the conversation.

For now, the hodler thesis is intact: Bitcoin took a half-billion-dollar hit to the ETF complex in two days and still closed November 4 near $78,230. That's not weakness. That's absorption.

What It Means

  • ETF sellers are losing control of price direction — $514.2 million in November outflows failed to push BTC into sustained breakdown territory, suggesting the non-ETF bid is structurally significant.
  • BlackRock's IBIT redemptions bear watching — at $95 million on a single day, IBIT is the dominant driver of outflow pressure; any reversal there would likely flip the overall ETF flow picture quickly.
  • $80,000 is the decisive level — a reclaim of that price, where the EMA200 sits at $80,812, would likely confirm the recovery as a trend resumption; failure there keeps downside risk alive.
  • The November 6 jobs report is the next catalyst — stronger-than-expected employment data could renew Federal Reserve hawkishness, adding macro headwind to already cautious institutional positioning in BTC.
