Bitcoin Crashes Below $68K: ETF Exodus, $1.6B in Liquidations Signal Deeper Pain Ahead

AITechWire Crypto Desk — November 14, 2026

Bitcoin is not simply correcting — it is unraveling in layers. ETF holders are walking, leveraged longs are being destroyed, and the macro backdrop is actively hostile; that combination makes this more than a routine pullback and raises a genuine question about where forced selling stops.

What Happened: A Cascade, Not a Dip

Bitcoin fell to $67,500 on November 12, its lowest level in nearly three months, extending month-to-date losses beyond 13%. The drop continued into November 13, with the price briefly touching $67,568 before staging a partial recovery toward the $68,000–$69,000 range. At the intraday low, Bitcoin's market capitalization slid below $1.38 trillion, dragging the broader cryptocurrency market's valuation to $2.4 trillion.

The derivatives market amplified everything. CoinGlass recorded approximately $1.47 billion in liquidations over a 24-hour window, with roughly $1.31 billion of that wiped from long positions. A single four-hour window saw $798 million in liquidated crypto positions, with long trades accounting for $718.5 million of that figure. Bitcoin.com's data, citing Bitstamp, put the broader liquidation figure at $1.62 billion, with long positions accounting for nearly 90%. By any measure, the leveraged-long community took a historic beating.

Altcoins offered no shelter. Ethereum fell below $2,000 and touched $1,910, losing more than 6% on the day. XRP briefly broke below the dollar mark, extending its weekly decline past 15%. Solana slid from above $83 to $78. Most major altcoins shed 4%–6%, with only Hyperliquid and TRX posting relatively modest losses of 0.8% and 2%, respectively.

Why It Matters: Three Pressure Points Converging at Once

The sell-off is not a single-cause event — it is three separate pressure systems hitting simultaneously, which is why recovery attempts keep failing.

Spot ETF outflows. According to Farside Investors, spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded $299.8 million in net outflows on November 11, followed by a preliminary $47.7 million on November 12. Across the six trading sessions from November 5 through November 12, total reported withdrawals reached approximately $1.49 billion. These are not retail panic sellers; this is institutional capital choosing the exit at scale. The Coinbase Premium Index, which had stood at 0.8 in September, fell below negative 4 — its average reading hitting the lowest level since February — confirming that US-based demand has genuinely dried up.

Leveraged long destruction. Outstanding Bitcoin futures positions had already exceeded $50 billion, raising liquidation risk around the $72,000 level even before the breakdown. Once Bitcoin broke below $72,000, then failed to hold key resistance, the cascade became self-reinforcing: falling prices triggered margin calls, which created more selling, which triggered more margin calls. Long positions accounted for 96% of Bitcoin-specific liquidations in one data set, meaning nearly every forced seller was a bull who had run out of runway.

Macro headwinds. October's hotter-than-expected inflation report strengthened expectations of another interest rate increase under Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh. Treasury yields above 5% have made dollar-denominated assets relatively more attractive, reducing the marginal appetite for speculative exposure like Bitcoin. Higher borrowing costs historically compress liquidity and punish leveraged risk assets first — exactly the dynamic playing out now.

Monthly spot demand stood at negative 280,000 BTC and perpetual futures demand at negative 82,000 BTC, combining for negative 362,000 BTC — a demand picture that is not ambiguous.

The Counter-View: Why Bulls Aren't Wrong to Watch $65K

The honest counter-argument is that every number cited above is a backward-looking signal. Liquidations of this magnitude historically flush out the weakest hands and can establish durable floors. When $1.47 billion in long positions are forcibly closed, those traders cannot sell again — the selling pressure from that cohort is exhausted. If institutional ETF outflows slow or reverse, the removal of that overhead could allow a sharper-than-expected bounce.

The technical picture frames the battlefield clearly. The $67,500–$68,000 zone is the first level to watch. A sustained break below that intraday low opens $66,000 and then the psychologically significant $65,000. Further weakness beyond that could bring $62,500–$63,000 into view. For bulls, the target is simpler: reclaim key resistance, which has flipped from support to resistance, and the character of this move changes entirely.

The open question is whether institutional ETF redemptions represent profit-taking after a strong year or a genuine re-rating of Bitcoin's risk profile in a higher-rate environment. The Coinbase Premium data suggests the latter is at least partly true, which makes a quick reversal harder to construct.

Resolution: Capitulation Risk Is Real, Not Rhetorical

Strategy's removal from MSCI indexes added another layer of uncertainty, raising concerns around corporate Bitcoin treasury companies broadly. The expected index-related selling affects Strategy shares rather than its underlying BTC holdings — but sentiment around corporate Bitcoin holders has curdled nonetheless, removing one of the market's most visible demand narratives.

What the data describes is a market where institutional demand is withdrawing, leverage is being violently repriced, and the macro environment is providing no tailwind. That is the definition of capitulation risk: not a single catastrophic event, but a grinding pressure that exhausts buyers faster than it exhausts sellers. Bitcoin at current levels is not cheap if the conditions that pushed it there have not changed.

What It Means

  • ETF holders face continued pressure — with $1.49 billion in outflows across six sessions and the Coinbase Premium Index at multi-month lows, institutional demand signals suggest further redemptions are more likely than a reversal in the near term.
  • Leveraged traders are the immediate losers — over $1.3 billion in long positions were forcibly liquidated; those positions are gone, but any surviving leveraged longs remain vulnerable if Bitcoin cannot hold the $67,500–$68,000 floor.
  • $65,000 is the level to watch — a sustained break below $67,500 likely opens that target; a reclaim of key resistance would materially change the technical picture and is the minimum bulls need to demonstrate the correction is exhausted.
  • Macro is not a tailwind — with Treasury yields above 5% and rate-hike expectations firming under Fed Chair Warsh, the environment that historically supports speculative risk assets remains absent; this likely means volatility stays elevated into year-end.
