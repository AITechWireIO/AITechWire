ETH/BTC rises 0.79% as Ethereum outperforms Bitcoin

ETH/BTC climbed to 0.02921 with ETH at $2,273 (+1.28%) vs BTC at $77,816 (+0.51%). Crypto market cap hit $2.705T amid relative strength in altcoins.

AI TechWire · November 01, 2026 · 4 min read
Photo by Traxer / Unsplash

ETH/BTC Climbs 0.79%: Is the Market Rotating Back Into Ethereum?

Ethereum is quietly reasserting itself against Bitcoin, and the ETH/BTC exchange rate is the clearest signal yet. Over the past 24 hours, that ratio climbed 0.79% to 0.02921 — a move small enough to ignore in isolation, but significant in what it implies about where smart money may be positioning next.

What Happened

The numbers are straightforward. Ethereum traded at $2,273, up 1.28% over 24 hours. Bitcoin, meanwhile, gained 0.51%, settling at $77,816. Both assets moved higher, but ETH's gain was more than double BTC's in percentage terms — and that relative performance is what the ETH/BTC ratio captures.

The broader market followed suit. Total cryptocurrency market capitalization rebounded to $2.705 trillion, rising 0.2% on the day, with 24-hour trading volume recorded at $68.206 billion. The direction is constructive: a market cap recovery paired with altcoin outperformance is a pattern that historically precedes broader altcoin seasons, though the open question is always whether such moves have legs or simply reflect a brief exhale after BTC-led compression.

Data cited by Lookonchain, drawing on HTX market figures, characterized Ethereum's rebound sentiment as "stronger than Bitcoin's" — a framing that goes beyond price arithmetic. Sentiment, in this context, means traders are actively choosing ETH over BTC at the margin, not merely riding a rising tide.

"Ethereum's rebound momentum is stronger than Bitcoin's, with the ETH/BTC exchange rate rising 0.79% over the past 24 hours."

- Lookonchain, citing HTX market data

Why This Move Matters

The ETH/BTC ratio is one of the most-watched relative-value signals in crypto markets. When it rises, it signals that capital is moving — or rotating — from the market's reserve asset into Ethereum, which functions as the base layer for the vast majority of decentralized finance, NFT infrastructure, and smart-contract activity across the ecosystem. A rising ETH/BTC ratio doesn't just mean ETH is doing well; it means ETH is doing better than the benchmark, which is a distinct and more meaningful claim.

For traders running Ethereum ecosystem plays — layer-2 tokens, DeFi protocols, ETH-denominated yield strategies — this is the number they watch first. It tells them whether the wind is at their back. When ETH outperforms BTC, the entire Ethereum stack tends to get a lift, because capital that rotates into ETH often continues flowing further out the risk curve into assets denominated in or correlated to it.

The market cap figure reinforces the case. A $2.705 trillion total market cap recovering 0.2% on the day, against a backdrop where ETH is outpacing BTC, suggests the rotation is broad rather than idiosyncratic to a single asset. Altcoins as a class appear to be gaining relative ground.

The Counter-View: One Day Does Not a Rotation Make

The bear case here is simple and worth stating plainly: a 0.79% move in ETH/BTC over 24 hours is noise until confirmed. Bitcoin is still the dominant reserve asset in crypto, trading near $77,816 with its own positive momentum. A half-percent gain in BTC is not a sign of weakness — it is a sign that BTC holders are not panicking, which historically makes it harder for altcoin rallies to sustain themselves. Rotation theories live and die on follow-through.

There is also the volume question. The $68.206 billion in 24-hour trading volume is a data point, not a verdict. Without knowing how that volume is distributed across assets — how much is ETH, how much is BTC, how much is everything else — it is impossible to say whether genuine rotation capital is flowing or whether ETH simply had a stronger day on lighter relative volume. The signal is real; the magnitude of what it signals remains genuinely uncertain.

That uncertainty is exactly why the ETH/BTC ratio needs to hold and extend over multiple sessions to carry analytical weight. A single-day outperformance is a hypothesis. Several days in a row is evidence.

The Resolution: Watch the Ratio, Not Just the Price

Here is what the data actually tells us today: Ethereum is in a stronger near-term position relative to Bitcoin than it was 24 hours ago, the overall crypto market is recovering rather than contracting, and the conditions for a broader altcoin move are at least present. That is a meaningful setup, even if it is not yet a confirmed trend.

The ETH/BTC rate at 0.02921 is the number to track. If it continues climbing over the next 48 to 72 hours, the rotation thesis gains credibility and Ethereum ecosystem plays — DeFi protocols, layer-2 infrastructure, ETH-collateralized strategies — become more attractive on a relative basis. If it gives back the 0.79% gain and BTC reasserts dominance, the move reads as a short-term relief bounce in a BTC-led market and not much more.

For now, Ethereum has the momentum. The question is whether the market agrees long enough to matter.

What It Means

  • ETH holders gain near-term confidence: Ethereum's 1.28% gain against Bitcoin's 0.51% advance, with ETH/BTC rising to 0.02921, marks a clear day of outperformance — this likely means short-term sentiment is favoring ETH over BTC at the margin.
  • Ethereum ecosystem assets are the ones to watch: When ETH outperforms BTC, correlated assets in DeFi and layer-2 infrastructure historically benefit; traders in those positions have the wind at their backs today.
  • The $2.705 trillion market cap recovery is constructive but narrow: A 0.2% rebound in total market cap is positive, but confirmation requires sustained volume above $68.206 billion and continued ETH/BTC strength over multiple sessions.
  • BTC at $77,816 is not retreating: Bitcoin's own positive performance means this is not a flight from BTC — it is a relative rotation, which is historically more durable than moves driven by BTC weakness alone.
