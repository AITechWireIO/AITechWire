Crypto ETFs See $430M Outflows Amid Rout

Spot Bitcoin funds recorded heavy redemptions post-tech selloff; leverage flush dominated trading. On-chain data shows miner and whale activity spikes.

AI TechWire · November 07, 2026 · 4 min read
Photo by Pierre Borthiry - Peiobty / Unsplash

Thursday's crypto selloff was not a blip — it was a stress test, and institutional holders blinked. A combined $530 million drained from US spot Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs in a single session, wiping out every gain logged since the midterm elections and exposing just how thin the conviction behind the recent rally actually was.

What Happened: A $530 Million Single-Day Flush

On November 5, US spot Bitcoin ETFs bled $430.23 million in net outflows while Ethereum funds shed another $99.98 million, according to data from Farside Investors. That one-day figure arrived on the heels of a brief $128 million inflow Wednesday — a recovery that evaporated almost immediately. The proximate trigger was brutal: the Nasdaq Composite plunged 5.5%, its steepest single-day drop since April 2025, dragging Bitcoin back below $76,000 from a brief perch above $80,000 reached earlier in the week.

The damage was broad. Not one of the 12 tracked US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded positive net flows on Thursday. Fidelity's Wise Origin Bitcoin Fund (FBTC) led all withdrawals with $199.87 million in outflows — a whiplash reversal from the $236.7 million it attracted just the day before. BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) added $105 million in outflows, extending what is now a five-consecutive-session losing streak. Since October 30, IBIT alone has seen approximately $1.04 billion pulled from the fund, including a single-session withdrawal of $405.3 million on November 2. Grayscale's GBTC lost another $49.9 million. Franklin Templeton's EZBC dropped $32.92 million. VanEck's HODL shed $23.64 million. ARK 21Shares' ARKB contributed $18.9 million to the outflow column.

Zoom out even slightly and the picture is starker: cumulative net withdrawals across all US spot Bitcoin ETFs reached approximately $1.51 billion over the five sessions since October 30. Year-to-date net outflows for Bitcoin ETFs now stand at around $510 million.

"I expect Nasdaq to bottom tomorrow. The same for Bitcoin, potentially after sweeping $75,000."

- Crypto analyst Michaël van de Poppe, writing on X, November 6, 2026

Why It Matters: Institutional Demand Is the Story Now

The post-midterm euphoria that briefly pushed Bitcoin above $80,000 has fully collapsed, and the mechanism of that collapse tells you something important about where crypto markets stand in late 2026. These are no longer markets driven purely by retail momentum. The ETF wrapper that was supposed to deliver durable, patient institutional capital is behaving instead like a risk-on/risk-off toggle, moving in near-lockstep with equities. When the Nasdaq falls 5.5% in a single day, $430 million exits Bitcoin ETFs by closing bell.

That correlation is the real signal here. Spot ETFs were sold to regulators, advisors, and investors partly on the premise that Bitcoin provides portfolio diversification. Thursday's trading suggests that thesis faces serious headwinds when macro fear dominates. Fidelity's FBTC attracting $236.7 million on Wednesday and then hemorrhaging $199.87 million on Thursday is not portfolio construction — it is momentum trading with an institutional face.

The macro backdrop adds pressure. The October jobs report, released Friday by the Bureau of Labor Statistics, showed nonfarm payrolls rising 165,000, well above the Wall Street forecast of 59,000, with unemployment nudging down to 4.1%. While that is unambiguously strong economic data, it has a complicating effect: traders raised the probability of another Federal Reserve rate hike when the FOMC meets December 8–9 to 76.8%, according to CME Group's FedWatch tool. A higher-for-longer rate environment is historically unfriendly to risk assets, and crypto sits near the top of the risk stack.

Source: Seema Shah, Chief Global Strategist, Principal Asset Management - "Today's jobs report is a strong rebuttal to fears that the economy is heading into a meaningful slowdown." link

That rebuttal cuts both ways. A strong economy might mean the selloff is not the beginning of something systemic — but it also means the Fed has room to keep tightening, which is exactly the kind of environment that tends to keep institutional crypto allocators cautious.

The Counter-View: This Is Not 2008

Not everyone is reading Thursday's action as the start of a deeper unwind. Dean Lyulkin, CEO of Cardiff and founder of investment publication The Dean's List, pushed back directly on the crisis comparisons circulating through markets.

Source: Dean Lyulkin, CEO of Cardiff - "The global financial crisis wasn't caused by Democrats taking Congress." link

Lyulkin's argument is structural: unlike the period preceding 2008, the Federal Reserve had already raised rates substantially and the housing market was weakening before the last crisis. Today's household balance sheets and banking system are, by his read, considerably stronger. He acknowledged vulnerabilities in commercial real estate, private credit, and potentially excessive AI spending — but stopped short of calling them systemic.

That framing matters because it sets a floor for how bearish the most thoughtful market observers are willing to get. Van de Poppe, for his part, telegraphed a near-term Bitcoin bottom potentially involving a sweep below $75,000 — painful, but recoverable. The open question is whether a December rate hike changes that calculus entirely.

Resolution: Where the Pressure Points Are

November's Bitcoin ETF outflows now total $869.8 million with weeks still to go in the month. IBIT's five-day streak above $1 billion in redemptions is the kind of number that gets quoted in institutional risk committees. The brief Wednesday recovery — $128 million in combined inflows — showed that buyers exist near these levels, but they are fast-moving and easily spooked by equity market volatility.

What comes next depends heavily on whether the Nasdaq stabilizes and whether December Fed pricing continues to tighten. If rate hike odds remain above 75% heading into month-end, the pressure on crypto ETF flows is unlikely to ease. If equities find footing, Wednesday's pattern — a sharp recovery inflow — could repeat. But the structural story has shifted: Bitcoin ETFs have now shed more than they have gained year-to-date, and the product's promise of steady institutional demand is on trial.

What It Means

  • Fidelity's FBTC and BlackRock's IBIT are now the clearest gauges of institutional sentiment; consecutive outflow sessions from both are the first warning sign to watch in any equity downturn.
  • With CME FedWatch pricing a 76.8% probability of a December rate hike, risk appetite across crypto is likely to remain compressed through the FOMC meeting on December 8–9.
  • Bitcoin holding above $75,000 is the near-term line; a sweep below that level, as Van de Poppe flagged, could accelerate ETF redemptions and deepen DeFi liquidity stress.
  • The Wednesday/Thursday whipsaw — $128 million in, $530 million out — signals that tactical buyers exist but are no substitute for the durable institutional demand the ETF ecosystem still needs to prove.
