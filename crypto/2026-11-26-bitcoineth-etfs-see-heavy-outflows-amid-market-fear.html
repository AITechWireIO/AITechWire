Bitcoin/ETH ETFs See Heavy Outflows Amid Market Fear

US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded ~$453M net outflows on Nov 25, continuing multi-day redemptions. Flows signal institutional caution despite BTC holding near $60k amid holiday trading.

AI TechWire · November 26, 2026 · 4 min read
Photo by Kanchanara / Unsplash

Institutions Are Voting With Their Feet — and Bitcoin Is Starting to Flinch

Seven straight days of outflows from US spot Bitcoin ETFs, capped by $453.12 million in a single session on November 25, is not a blip — it is a signal. Even as BTC rebounded above $60,000 the following morning, the institutional money pulling out of these funds is making the rally harder to trust and easier to fade.

What Happened: A Historic Month of Redemptions

November is shaping up as the worst month on record for Bitcoin ETFs. Cumulative net outflows have climbed to roughly $5.39 billion, already exceeding any previous monthly total with trading sessions still remaining. The November 25 session alone ranked as the fourth-biggest single-day outflow this month, with $453.12 million leaving across the fund complex.

The damage was not evenly distributed. BlackRock's IBIT led the exits with $329.71 million redeemed in a single day. Grayscale's BTC vehicle shed $60.12 million, Ark & 21Shares ARKB lost $14.90 million, Grayscale's GBTC dropped $13.92 million, Bitwise BITB lost $18.21 million, VanEck HODL gave up $9.74 million, and Fidelity's FBTC shed $6.51 million. Out of 12 providers, seven recorded outflows and not one saw net inflows on the day. Total net assets across Bitcoin ETFs now stand at $72.03 billion — 5.94% of Bitcoin's total market cap — while cumulative net inflow has fallen to $51.59 billion.

Ethereum ETFs compounded the picture. November 25 marked a ninth consecutive day of outflows, with $68.51 million leaving — all of it from BlackRock's ETHA fund. Every other Ethereum ETF provider held flat. Cumulative monthly outflows for Ethereum funds now sit at $1.3 billion, with total net assets at $8.87 billion, representing 4.29% of Ethereum's market cap.

Prices, meanwhile, told a stranger story. Bitcoin fell to around $57,300 during the session before rebounding 2.6% to trade at $60,525 early Thursday. Ethereum gained 2.3% to $1,689.08. BNB rose 2.4% and XRP added 3.8%. Spot buyers, it seems, are not reading from the same playbook as ETF holders.

Why It Matters: Institutions Are De-Risking Into Year-End

The divergence between ETF outflows and spot price gains is the central tension right now, and analysts are reading it as a sign of professional caution rather than retail panic.

Source: Dominick John, Zeus Research — "Persistent outflows weaken spot demand, increasing downside risks and making sustained rallies more difficult." link

"Institutions remain focused on capital preservation until fund flows stabilize."

- Dan Khus, LVRG Research

Justin d'Anethan of Arctic Digital attributed much of the volatility to macroeconomic forces hitting risk assets broadly — not just crypto. That framing matters: when selling pressure is macro-driven rather than crypto-specific, even positive on-chain developments struggle to hold a rally. Proposed US Section 301 tariffs and expectations around a December interest rate decision have added to the pressure, cooling appetite for high-beta assets across both equity and digital markets. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index dropped to 7, squarely in "extreme fear" territory.

Galaxy's Alex Thorn went further, withdrawing his firm's previous view that Bitcoin's bear market had ended. Thorn warned that a move below a key price level could open the door to a lower low — a meaningful shift in tone from one of the market's more closely watched research voices.

The Counter-View: Spot Buyers and Corporate Accumulators Haven't Left

Here is where the story complicates itself. The same day ETFs bled $453 million, Bitcoin-backed AI company Next Technology (NXTT) sold 500 BTC to fund AI development but still holds 5,333 BTC on its balance sheet. Strategy's Michael Saylor used the moment to announce ambitions to buy all 900,000 Bitcoin still left to be mined, claiming the firm has purchased every Bitcoin mined over the last four years.

These statements appear to have produced a split: some ETF holders used the Saylor headlines as a profit-taking signal, while spot buyers used the same headlines to bid prices higher. That dynamic — institutional funds selling into strength while corporate accumulators and retail absorb supply — is what has kept Bitcoin elevated rather than testing levels significantly lower.

XRP adds another wrinkle. XRP ETFs turned net positive for the first time in November, logging $4.07 million in inflows led by Franklin Templeton. XRP itself climbed 4.1% to $0.9524. It is a small data point, but it hints that institutional capital is rotating within the digital asset complex rather than abandoning it entirely.

One Ethereum whale made the rotation dynamic visible in a single afternoon: a wallet sold 17,183 ETH at $1,669 on November 25, then bought back 16,984 ETH at $1,691 roughly two hours later — a round trip that cost 199 ETH. That is not an exit. That is active positioning.

Resolution: Flows Are the Leading Indicator, Prices Are the Lagging One

The open question is whether the outflow streak breaks before prices do. Research firms point to clearer Federal Reserve signals, cooling inflation data, or a return of institutional demand as the most likely catalysts for reversing ETF trends. Stablecoin growth has also been flagged as a secondary indicator worth watching — fresh stablecoin issuance historically precedes renewed buying pressure in spot markets.

Until inflows return, every price rally faces structural headwinds. Bitcoin ETFs with $72 billion in net assets represent a large and liquid exit door. When professional allocators choose to use it at scale — as they clearly have this month — the burden of proof falls on buyers to demonstrate they can absorb that supply and push prices materially higher.

What It Means

  • Bitcoin ETF holders — particularly those in BlackRock's IBIT — are the clearest losers this month, with $329.71 million redeemed in a single session and cumulative November outflows at $5.39 billion; sustained redemptions at this pace reduce the structural bid that drove prices higher earlier this year.
  • XRP and its ETF investors are the relative winners in the short term, with Franklin Templeton-led inflows marking the first positive day for XRP ETFs in November — this likely signals tactical rotation, not a broad sentiment shift.
  • A December interest rate decision and incoming inflation data are the most immediate catalysts to watch; analysts across Zeus Research, LVRG, and Arctic Digital all flag macro clarity as the prerequisite for ETF flow stabilization.
  • Corporate accumulators like Strategy and NXTT are absorbing supply that ETFs are releasing — if that dynamic holds, the downside may be limited, but it also caps the upside until institutional fund flows reverse.
