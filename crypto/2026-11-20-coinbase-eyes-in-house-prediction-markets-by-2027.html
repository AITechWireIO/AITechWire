Coinbase Eyes In-House Prediction Markets by 2027

Coinbase plans proprietary event contracts launch H1 2027 after Kalshi success ($200M ARR); supports CFTC swap inclusion for sports/politics/weather. Senate advances DEATH BETS Act restrictions.

AI TechWire · November 20, 2026 · 4 min read
Photo by PiggyBank / Unsplash

Coinbase's Prediction Market Bet Is a Smart Move Into a Sector Finding Its Legs — If the Regulatory Ground Holds

Prediction markets have graduated from crypto curiosity to legitimate financial infrastructure, and Coinbase wants a seat at the table before the chair gets pulled away. The exchange's reported plans to launch proprietary event contracts in the first half of 2027 track almost perfectly with a broader market moment: rising institutional interest in event-based trading, an emboldened CFTC under new leadership, and a legislative environment that is simultaneously opening one door and slamming another.

The timing is not accidental. It is strategic — and the macro backdrop of late November 2026 makes the stakes sharper than the headlines suggest.

The Market Moment Coinbase Is Reading

Kalshi's rise is the proof of concept Coinbase needed. The prediction market platform has demonstrated that retail and institutional appetite for event contracts — covering everything from sports outcomes to political results to weather events — is real and scalable. Coinbase is now signaling it wants to build its own version of that infrastructure rather than cede the category to a standalone fintech.

The logic is straightforward: exchanges that diversify revenue streams away from pure spot and derivatives trading are more resilient in volatile conditions. That resilience matters enormously right now. Bitcoin slid below $62,000 this week, its lowest level since early July, with spot Bitcoin ETFs recording $582.2 million in net outflows on Thursday alone — their largest single-day exodus since October 30. November's total ETF outflows have climbed to nearly $3.5 billion, and total net assets in those funds have fallen to $76.6 billion.

Source: Neil Steinhardt, Head of Nexo U.S. - "Within crypto, the most telling sign is this month's outflow from spot Bitcoin ETFs, which suggests investors are choosing to stay on the sidelines for now." link

When your core trading revenue is tied to an asset that just formed a death cross and triggered $727 million in liquidations across crypto markets in a single 24-hour window — with more than 151,000 traders liquidated — the case for building adjacent revenue lines becomes existential, not optional.

Why Prediction Markets Are the Right Adjacent Bet

Event contracts offer something that spot crypto trading does not: revenue that is structurally decoupled from Bitcoin's price. Whether BTC is at $61,000 or $91,000, people will want to trade on election outcomes, championship results, and macroeconomic data prints. That non-correlation is the product's most attractive feature for an exchange trying to smooth out earnings volatility.

The CFTC angle is equally important. With Michael Selig now serving as CFTC Chairman — having been sworn in December 2025 and stepping into a chair vacated by acting Chair Caroline Pham — the regulatory posture toward event contracts has shifted meaningfully. Coinbase's reported support for the CFTC's potential inclusion of sports, politics, and weather contracts under the swap framework signals that the exchange is positioning itself not just as a product builder but as a regulatory ally. That is a playbook Coinbase has used before: get ahead of the rule, shape the rule, benefit from the rule.

"Because the pressure appears tied to the rate backdrop, and that backdrop has shifted before, a turn in yields or the dollar would likely be the first signal that appetite is returning."

- Neil Steinhardt, Head of Nexo U.S.

CoinShares expects Bitcoin to trade between $60,000 and $90,000 for much of 2027. That is a wide band — and in a wide band, exchanges make less money on directional trading. Prediction markets fill the gap. They monetize uncertainty itself rather than directional conviction, which means the product actually performs better in exactly the kind of choppy, range-bound environment analysts are forecasting.

The Counter-Force: Legislative Risk Is Real

The Senate's movement on the DEATH BETS Act represents the clearest threat to this entire category, and it would be a mistake to dismiss it as political theater. Legislation restricting event contracts — particularly those tied to mortality, elections, or sensitive social outcomes — has found bipartisan traction before, and the political optics of "betting on death" are not a fight any exchange wins in a news cycle.

The open question is how broadly such restrictions would be drawn. A narrowly written law targeting a specific class of morbid contracts might leave Coinbase's planned product largely untouched. A broadly written one, particularly if it captures political or health-outcome markets, could gut the addressable market before the first contract clears.

The Federal Reserve's rate trajectory adds a second layer of risk. With the Fed signaling further rate hikes may be necessary, and Treasury yields sitting above 5%, the cost of capital for building out new product infrastructure is not trivial. The 10-year yield eased three basis points to 5.07% on Thursday — but that remains restrictive by any recent historical standard. The December 4 jobs report and the Fed's December 9 meeting are the next checkpoints that will either confirm or complicate the rate story heading into the year that Coinbase is targeting for launch.

Resolution: The Window Is Open, But Not Wide

Coinbase is making a calculated bet that the regulatory window for event contracts will be wide enough in H1 2027 to justify moving first. The CFTC's current leadership posture supports that read. The legislative risk is real but not yet determinative. And the macro environment — rate pressure, Bitcoin range-trading, ETF outflows — is actively making the case that revenue diversification is not a luxury.

The company that cracks scalable, compliant prediction market infrastructure for a retail crypto audience will own a category that has no dominant exchange-native player yet. That is what Coinbase is actually betting on. The question is whether the regulatory runway holds long enough for them to land.

What it means

  • Coinbase's move into event contracts is primarily a revenue diversification play — driven by the kind of sustained Bitcoin ETF outflows and crypto market volatility visible in November 2026 data, this likely means other major exchanges will face pressure to follow or cede the category.
  • CFTC Chairman Michael Selig's posture is the single most important variable: a pro-expansion ruling on swap inclusion for sports, politics, and weather contracts would be a green light; a reversal or departure would reset the timeline entirely.
  • The DEATH BETS Act is the structural wildcard — watch how broadly Senate drafters define restricted contract types; a wide definition could significantly constrain the addressable market before Coinbase's H1 2027 target.
  • CoinShares' forecast of Bitcoin trading between $60,000 and $90,000 through much of 2027 implicitly strengthens the prediction market thesis: range-bound crypto favors non-directional revenue, and exchanges that have it built will show it in margins first.
