Bitcoin ETFs See First Inflow in 7 Days

US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded a $7.9M net inflow on Nov 16 after seven straight outflow days, led by BlackRock IBIT.

AI TechWire · November 17, 2026 · 4 min read
Photo by Kanchanara / Unsplash

Bitcoin ETFs Break Seven-Day Outflow Streak — But One Fund Is Carrying the Whole Weight

The drought is over — technically. U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs posted a $7.9 million net inflow on November 16, snapping a seven-session run of outflows. But strip out BlackRock's IBIT, and nearly every other fund in the complex was still bleeding, raising a sharper question than the headline number suggests: is this stabilization, or is it BlackRock versus the field?

What Happened

On November 16, 2026, U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded a combined net inflow of $7.9 million — the first positive day in seven trading sessions. The number sounds modest against the scale of the market, and it is. But the composition of that number is where the story lives.

BlackRock's IBIT drove the entire reversal and then some, pulling in $76.5 million on the day. Without that single fund, the rest of the ETF complex would have posted a sizable net outflow. Fidelity's FBTC shed $11.3 million. Grayscale's GBTC, the legacy converted trust that has been a persistent source of sell pressure since launch, dropped another $34.6 million. ARK's ARKB lost $4.3 million, Bitwise's BTCO gave back $6.5 million, Grayscale's BTC product saw a $6.6 million outflow, EZBC dropped $3.8 million, and Morgan Stanley's MSBT posted a $1.5 million outflow. VanEck's HODL and WisdomTree's BTCW were flat.

The aggregate across the last five trading days still sits at negative $856.4 million. The cumulative net flow since October 14, 2025 stands at negative $9.67 billion. The ETFs collectively hold 641,515 BTC — a number that has shrunk by roughly 33,347 BTC over the past 30 days. The Fear and Greed index sits at 18, deep in Extreme Fear territory. This is not a market that feels rescued.

Why It Matters — And Why IBIT Is the Only Name That Does

BlackRock's dominance here is not incidental. IBIT absorbing $76.5 million on a day when virtually every competing product was in net redemption tells you something specific about where institutional conviction is concentrating. It is not spreading across the ETF landscape — it is consolidating at the top of the AUM hierarchy. For allocators watching this space, the signal is less about broad recovery and more about concentration in the leading fund.

That matters for allocation decisions in a direct way. Portfolio managers benchmarking to the broader ETF complex will see one green day and potentially interpret it as a turning point. But a fund-level read tells a different story: FBTC, GBTC, ARKB, and BTCO are all still in sustained redemption. The Grayscale products in particular continue to function as slow-motion liquidation vehicles — GBTC alone has shed $218.6 million over the past 30 days and $263.1 million over 90 days.

Ethereum spot ETFs are faring worse. On the same day, U.S. spot Ethereum ETFs posted a net outflow of $8.4 million, with Fidelity's FETH down $5 million, Grayscale's ETHE down $4.4 million, and only Grayscale's Mini ETH registering a modest $1 million inflow. If Bitcoin's one green day required a $76.5 million IBIT anchor to get there, Ethereum's products didn't even have that.

The Counter-Case: One Positive Day Does Not Rewrite the Trend

The bear case on this data is straightforward, and it deserves a full hearing. Seven consecutive days of outflows followed by a single $7.9 million net inflow — driven almost entirely by one fund — is not evidence of institutional demand stabilizing. It is evidence of BlackRock holding its ground while the rest of the market continues to exit. The last 30-day cumulative flow across all ETFs is negative $2.34 billion. The last 90 days are worse. Bitcoin's price as of these reports is hovering near recent levels, and the broader sentiment gauge is at extreme fear.

The pattern here fits a familiar playbook from prior ETF cycles: a dominant product absorbs inflows during risk-off periods not because demand is growing but because it is the last safe harbor — the fund large enough and liquid enough that institutional holders move to it rather than out of the asset class entirely. That is not the same as a floor forming. It is market share consolidation under stress.

The open question is whether IBIT's consistent ability to absorb flows — even during periods of broad outflows across the complex — eventually becomes a stabilizing force in its own right, or whether it simply delays a larger reckoning when redemptions finally hit the category leader directly.

Resolution: Watch the Next Four Sessions

One day ends a streak, but it does not set a new one. The ETF inflow streak currently sits at exactly one day. With the five-day average still deeply negative and sentiment at extreme fear, the next four trading sessions will tell the actual story. If IBIT can sustain inflows while the outflow pace at Grayscale and Fidelity moderates, the case for genuine stabilization becomes more credible. If IBIT's November 16 print was an isolated absorb rather than the start of a rotation back in, the aggregate numbers will return to negative quickly.

The BTC held by ETFs — 641,515 coins — is the number to track alongside the daily flow. If that figure stabilizes or rises while flows turn positive across more than one fund, the institutional demand story changes. Until then, the headline masks what is still a one-fund show.

What It Means

  • BlackRock wins regardless: IBIT is capturing flows — and potentially market share — even as competitor funds bleed; this likely means its dominance in the institutional Bitcoin ETF space widens further during the current downturn.
  • Grayscale remains the pressure point: GBTC's $34.6 million single-day outflow and $263.1 million 90-day outflow make it the clearest structural drag on aggregate ETF flows; watch whether its pace of redemption slows.
  • Ethereum ETFs have no equivalent anchor: With ETH products posting a net outflow of $8.4 million on the same day BTC turned positive, the case for institutional Ethereum demand through the ETF wrapper is significantly weaker right now.
  • The streak counter resets to one: Sustained inflow recovery would require multiple consecutive positive days across more than just IBIT — investors should not read a single $7.9 million net day against a negative $856.4 million five-day backdrop as confirmation of a trend change.
