Bitcoin drops toward key support after Fed dissent and weak tech earnings

Macro friction meets institutional infrastructure push.

AI TechWire · October 30, 2026 · 4 min read
Photo by Kanchanara / Unsplash

Bitcoin's drop to $80,000 is not a crypto story — it's a macro story wearing crypto's clothes. A hawkish split at the Federal Reserve, earnings misses from Alphabet and Samsung, and $356 million in futures liquidations converged in a single session to test one of the market's most-watched psychological levels. The bulls got two chances to reclaim momentum this week and fumbled both of them.

What Happened: Two Catalysts, Zero Lift

The Federal Open Market Committee held rates at 3.75%–4.00% on Wednesday in a 10-2 vote. The headline number was steady, but the dissent told a different story: two policymakers voted for an immediate 25-basis-point hike. Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh made no effort to close the door on December action.

"While overall financial conditions are tighter, I'm unwilling and unable to call them tight."

- Kevin Warsh, Federal Reserve Chair

Markets moved fast. The CME FedWatch Tool reset to reflect an 83% probability of a rate increase in December. Then came the earnings. Alphabet fell roughly 9%–10% in after-hours trade after Google Services revenue of $96.3 billion missed analyst expectations of $98.4 billion — heavy AI spending pushed free cash flow negative. Samsung's disappointment pulled Asian markets lower, with the KOSPI falling 1.20%. Bitcoin, which had briefly climbed above $81,000 following the Fed announcement and touched $81,700, collapsed back under $80,000 when Alphabet's results hit. By Thursday it was trading around $79,850.

Thursday's economic data did nothing to ease pressure. The preliminary estimate for third-quarter US GDP came in at an annualized 3.4%, beating the 3.2% forecast. Core PCE inflation registered 3.1% year-on-year. Strong growth plus sticky inflation is exactly the mix that makes the Fed's hawks look justified — and risk assets look expensive.

Why It Matters: Old Support Becomes Resistance

The $80,000 level is not arbitrary. It held as support for weeks before this break. When a price level that absorbed selling for an extended period finally cracks and then fails to be reclaimed on a retest, technical traders treat it as flipped resistance. Bitcoin had two shots at reclaiming that level — the Fed decision and big-tech earnings — and failed on both. That sequential failure is what separates a routine dip from a structural signal.

The $356 million in crypto futures liquidations that accompanied the move confirms the market was leaning long into these catalysts. Traders positioned for a dovish Fed surprise or an earnings-driven risk rally got squeezed out instead. That positioning overhang takes time to clear. Until it does, any bounce into the $80,000–$81,000 zone is more likely to attract sellers than buyers.

The broader implication is about timing. Bitcoin has spent much of 2026 trying to establish itself as a macro-independent asset — a store of value that trades on its own fundamentals rather than following the NASDAQ tick-for-tick. This week's action complicates that narrative. The correlation between Bitcoin's move and the Alphabet selloff was near-simultaneous, and the GDP and PCE prints that cemented rate-hike bets landed at almost the same moment Bitcoin lost $80,000. The relationship between tech equity sentiment and digital asset prices remains the defining variable for any short-term directional view.

Source: Kitco / Cassius Cuvée - "Bitcoin had two shots at real momentum this week, the Fed and big tech earnings, and couldn't use either one to reclaim it. This is not a good sign for the bulls." link

The Counter-View: Institutions Are Still Buying the Dip

The bear case is clean, but it is not uncontested. Despite the violent spot price move, US spot Bitcoin ETFs maintained positive net inflows across the week. Institutional capital kept flowing in even as retail futures traders were being liquidated. That divergence matters. It suggests the entities with the longest time horizons — pension allocators, multi-asset funds, family offices running through ETF wrappers — did not interpret $80,000 as a reason to exit. They interpreted it as a discount.

Asset manager Bitwise added a long-range data point to the bullish side of the ledger, revising its valuation target for Bitcoin to $1.6 million by 2036, citing structural compounding. That figure is a decade out and carries all the uncertainty that entails, but its publication during a week of acute selling pressure is a deliberate signal about where patient capital is oriented.

Microsoft also reported earnings this week and beat, with Azure growing 48% and shares rising about 4% after hours. That result demonstrates that not all big tech is bleeding — and a healthier earnings season broadly would remove one of the two triggers that drove this week's crypto selloff.

Resolution: Three Events That Settle the Debate

The next few sessions carry outsized weight. Amazon reports after the bell on Thursday, and its numbers will either extend the tech earnings damage or begin to heal it. More than $11 billion in Bitcoin options expire on Deribit on Friday — that expiry will accelerate whichever direction the market picks into the weekend. Apple reports Monday. US midterms fall on Tuesday. That is a genuinely compressed catalyst window, and the price action around $79,100 — the low from the prior weekend flagged by traders as the next structural support — will tell most of what needs to be known about whether this is a shakeout or the beginning of a deeper correction toward $75,000.

The Fed's December meeting is the longer-duration risk. With hike probability at 83% and both growth and inflation running hot, the policy path is no longer a coin flip. Bitcoin will have to prove it can hold $80,000 in an environment where the cost of capital is still moving higher.

What It Means

  • Bears hold the near-term edge: Failure to reclaim $80,000 on two separate catalysts flips that level to resistance; the $79,100 prior low is the next line, with $75,000 on the table if it breaks.
  • Institutional inflows are the key counterweight: Spot Bitcoin ETF demand staying positive through the dip likely prevents a freefall — watch weekly flow data as the clearest signal of institutional conviction.
  • Amazon and Apple earnings this week carry direct crypto implications: A broad tech recovery removes one of the two triggers that caused this selloff; a second wave of misses would compound selling pressure on digital assets.
  • December Fed policy is now the macro anchor: An 83% implied probability of a rate hike means Bitcoin's path above $81,000 likely requires either a significant inflation surprise or a shift in Fed communication before year-end.
