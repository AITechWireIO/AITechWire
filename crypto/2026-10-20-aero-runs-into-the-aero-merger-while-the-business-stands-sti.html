AERO Runs Into the Aero Merger While the Business Stands Still

AERO is up about a third in 30 days ahead of Wednesday's Aerodrome-Velodrome merger, yet TVL sits below its 5 Oct peak and volume is flat.

AERO Runs Into the Merger While the Business Stands Still

The token is up a third in a month. The protocol it represents is not. AERO's 33% rally into Wednesday's Aerodrome-Velodrome merger has priced in a transformation that has not yet happened — total value locked sits 11% below its October 5 peak, three of the seven launch chains show zero TVL today, and 90% of the existing business is still just Base. Tomorrow's 7:00 PM CT switch has to deliver new volume, new chains, and new users. A new token alone will not justify what the market has already paid.

The Rally That Got Ahead of the Business

AERO closed at $0.796 on October 19, down about 15% from its October 16 close of $0.937, before bouncing to roughly $0.87 by mid-morning on October 20. The 30-day gain is 33.7% by CoinGecko's figure, driven almost entirely by event anticipation rather than improving fundamentals.

The clearest illustration: AERO jumped 16% on October 11 after Dromos Labs co-founder and CEO Alexander Cutler told the Digital Asset Summit he expected "a lot of fireworks" from the relaunch. Within a day, those gains had been pared to about 8%.

"I think it's going to be a lot of fireworks."

— Alexander Cutler, CEO and Co-Founder, Dromos Labs, at the Digital Asset Summit, October 11, 2026

At $0.87, AERO carries a market cap of about $870 million and a fully diluted value of $1.73 billion. Seven-day swap fees across the combined protocol were $2.66 million. Annualizing that produces roughly $138 million against a market cap of $870 million — about six times annualized swap fees. That is not a distressed valuation; it already assumes meaningful growth from here.

Total value locked across the combined protocol is $400 million today, down from $412.8 million at the close of October 19 and 11% below the $450.5 million peak reached on October 5. The all-time TVL high was $1.355 billion in December 2024, the same month AERO hit its all-time price high of $2.32.

The Seven-Chain Story Needs Work

The merger's marquee pitch is a unified DEX launching across seven chains: Base, Ethereum mainnet, Robinhood Chain, ARC, OP Mainnet, Arbitrum, and Ink. In practice, DefiLlama's data as of October 20 shows Ethereum, Arbitrum, and Robinhood Chain at $0 TVL. Ink holds $7.2 million and ARC $3.4 million. OP Mainnet contributes $27.5 million. Base carries $361.3 million — 90% of everything.

Cutler addressed the skepticism at the Digital Asset Summit. When asked whether launching on six new chains put Aero at a disadvantage against established competitors, he pushed back: Aerodrome was not the first DEX on Base either. It started with just a few million dollars in deposits and grew to between 60% and 70% of Base's on-chain activity. His argument is that liquidity compounds — win the liquidity, win the trades, win the fees, reinvest the revenue, repeat.

That logic held on Base. Whether it holds simultaneously across six additional chains is the open question. Velodrome's weekly swap fees illustrate the gap: $30,000 on V2 and $72,000 on V3 over the past seven days, against Aerodrome's millions. The Velodrome side of this merger is a small business being absorbed into a larger one, receiving a 5.5% allocation of new supply in exchange.

What the Conversion Actually Does to Supply

Existing AERO converts 1:1 to new AERO. VELO converts at approximately 0.044 new AERO per token. With VELO's total supply at 2.648 billion, that adds roughly 116 million new AERO tokens — taking total supply from about 2.002 billion to approximately 2.12 billion, a roughly 6% increase.

VELO is currently trading at about a 2% discount to its conversion value: $0.0375 per token versus the implied conversion value of $0.0383 (0.044 multiplied by AERO's $0.8707 price). That gap is narrow enough to suggest the market sees the merger as credible but not so tight as to imply certainty.

The conversion is not automatic. Holders of self-custodied AERO and VELO must process tokens through an upgrade portal — after claiming outstanding rewards and rebases and clearing active votes. Existing veAERO and veVELO positions become sAERO, with remaining lock periods carried over and rounded up to the nearest week. Permanently locked positions convert to the longest available staking term. Weekly voting cycles are replaced by real-time allocation with a 47-hour cooldown on initial changes. Emissions on both legacy platforms stop at launch, and the project claims the new protocol will stream 100% of exchange revenue directly to AERO stakers.

One historical risk is worth naming plainly: in November 2025, the Aerodrome and Velodrome front ends were hit by a DNS hijack around the time of the merger announcement, with about $700,000 affected. The teams said contracts were untouched, but fake migration sites have already appeared once in this protocol's history. Any holder moving through an upgrade portal tomorrow should verify URLs with unusual care.

Key Insights

  • The rally is priced for growth, not the current business. AERO is up 33% in 30 days while TVL is 11% below its October 5 peak; a market cap of ~$870 million against ~$138 million in annualized swap fees means the market is paying for expansion that has not yet arrived.
  • The seven-chain narrative starts largely from zero. Three of the seven launch chains — Ethereum, Arbitrum, and Robinhood — show $0 TVL today. Base alone accounts for 90% of protocol value.
  • Supply increases by roughly 6% at launch. VELO's conversion adds approximately 116 million new AERO tokens; dilution is modest but real, and it begins the moment the portal opens.
  • Exchange mechanics create near-term friction. Binance suspends old-AERO deposits and withdrawals on November 3; VELODROME/USDT is removed the same day. A Coinbase migration has been reported for early November but is not confirmed. Holders not paying attention to timing could face access issues.
  • The DNS hijack precedent makes portal security a live concern. Fake migration sites are a documented risk in this protocol's history. One incident in November 2025 affected roughly $700,000. The upgrade portal is the single largest attack surface at launch.

What to Expect

  • Tomorrow at 7:00 PM CT, the Aero protocol goes live across seven chains, emissions on Aerodrome and Velodrome stop, and upgrade portals open. Holders will need to claim rewards, clear votes, and process conversions manually — the switch is not automatic, and the queue could be significant in the first hours.
  • Supply expands almost immediately. As VELO holders convert at the ~0.044 rate, approximately 116 million new AERO tokens enter circulation. If existing AERO holders simultaneously unlock and sell, the combination of new supply and event-driven profit-taking could pressure price even if the launch goes smoothly.
  • The real test is volume on the new chains over the following weeks. If Base dominance persists at 90% and Ethereum, Arbitrum, and Robinhood remain near zero, the seven-chain pitch will look like a roadmap rather than a launch — and the current multiple of roughly six times annualized fees will be hard to defend.
  • Binance's November 3 cut-off for legacy AERO and VELO is the next hard deadline after launch. Holders on centralized exchanges who have not converted by then may face suspended deposits and withdrawals; the new-AERO deposit date on Binance is still to be announced, creating a window of uncertainty for exchange-held positions.
