AERO Runs Into the Aero Merger While the Business Stands Still

AERO is up about a third in 30 days ahead of Wednesday's Aerodrome-Velodrome merger, yet TVL sits below its 5 Oct peak and volume is flat.

AI TechWire · October 20, 2026 · 4 min read
Photo by Nopparuj Lamaikul / Unsplash

AERO Has Run a Third Into a Merger the Business Hasn't Earned Yet

AERO is up about 33% over the last 30 days with the Aerodrome-Velodrome merger going live tomorrow evening. The problem is that the protocol it represents a claim on hasn't grown to match the price — total value locked is sitting 11% below its October 5 peak, three of the seven chains the new Aero protocol is launching on show zero TVL today, and 90% of the combined liquidity is still concentrated on a single chain. The launch has to bring new volume. A new token is not enough.

What the price run actually reflects

At $0.87 as of Tuesday morning, AERO is still roughly 63% below its all-time high of $2.32 set in December 2024. The 30-day rally of about 33% is largely a merger premium, not a reaction to business improvement. AERO's daily close peaked at $0.943 on October 12, then fell roughly 15% through October 19 before bouncing on the eve of the launch. That pattern — a run-up, a fade, then a pre-event bounce — suggests traders are pricing in the narrative more than any measurable acceleration in underlying activity.

On October 11, co-founder Alexander Cutler told the Digital Asset Summit the launch would be "a lot of fireworks," describing it as the protocol's "third DEX OS" and calling it "the most ambitious one we have yet." AERO jumped 16% that day, then surrendered half the move within 24 hours. Sentiment on Stocktwits, which tracked AERO as a top trending ticker that day, shifted from bullish to bearish and from extremely high chatter to low within a single session. The market heard the pitch and then asked for evidence.

"A DEX is powered by liquidity. If you win liquidity, you win more trades, you win more fees, you win more revenue, and then you can inject that value right back into the system."

- Alexander Cutler, Aerodrome co-founder, Digital Asset Summit, October 11, 2026

The business underneath hasn't kept pace

Strip away the merger story and the underlying numbers are flat to slightly weaker. Combined TVL for the Aero protocol stands at $400 million as of Tuesday morning, down from $412.8 million at the end of October 19 and down 11% from the 30-day high of $450.5 million reached on October 5. The all-time TVL high was $1.355 billion, set in December 2024.

The chain concentration is the number that should give a buyer pause. Base holds $361.3 million — 90% of total TVL. OP Mainnet contributes $27.5 million, Ink adds $7.2 million, and Arc sits at $3.4 million. Ethereum, Arbitrum, and Robinhood Chain — three of the seven chains in the headline launch — show zero TVL today.

Cutler's answer is that Aerodrome never had a structural head start on Base either, starting from just a few million dollars before growing to capture 60% to 70% of Base's on-chain activity. Slipstream, the concentrated liquidity product, now accounts for $201 million of TVL and roughly 63% of Base DEX volume on a given day. The seven-day swap fee figure of $2.66 million annualizes to about $138 million against a market cap of roughly $870 million — approximately six times annualized swap fees. Whether that multiple is cheap or full depends entirely on whether the new chains generate fresh volume or simply redistribute existing liquidity.

Velodrome's contribution is a useful reality check. Velodrome V2 generated about $30,000 in fees over the last seven days; V3 added about $72,000. The new AERO token will represent claims on a revenue pool that is, today, almost entirely an Aerodrome story.

The supply math and the exchange mechanics

The current AERO circulating supply is approximately 1 billion, with a total supply of 2.002 billion. Converting VELO at the announced ratio of roughly 0.044 new AERO per VELO — applied to VELO's total supply of 2.648 billion — adds approximately 116 million new AERO, bringing total supply to about 2.12 billion, a roughly 6% increase. Aerodrome holders end up with about 94.5% of the new supply; Velodrome holders receive the remaining 5.5%.

The conversion is not automatic. Holders of self-custodied AERO and VELO must process the swap through upgrade portals on Aerodrome or Velodrome after claiming rewards and rebases and clearing active votes. Existing veAERO and veVELO positions convert into staked sAERO, with remaining lock periods carried over and rounded up to the nearest week. Weekly voting is replaced by real-time allocation, subject to a 47-hour cooldown on initial changes.

Exchange timing adds another variable. Binance has announced that on November 3, the VELODROME/USDT pair will be removed and open orders cancelled; old-AERO deposits and withdrawals are suspended at 11:30 that morning (UTC+8). The date for new-AERO deposits on Binance is yet to be announced. One secondary source reported a Coinbase migration in the November 2 to 4 window, though that has not been independently confirmed. Traders holding on centralized exchanges who do nothing face a window where withdrawal functionality may be limited.

Key insights

  • The rally is a premium, not a result. AERO is up about 33% in 30 days, but TVL is 11% below its October 5 peak. That gap needs to close through new volume, not further anticipation.
  • Three of seven chains are at zero. Ethereum, Arbitrum, and Robinhood Chain show no TVL today. Whether Aero can replicate on those chains what it built on Base is the only question that justifies a higher price.
  • Velodrome is a small add-on, not a doubling. Velodrome's weekly swap fees are roughly $102,000 combined across V2 and V3, a minor addition to Aerodrome's $2.66 million weekly fee base. VELO holders receive 5.5% of the new supply, pricing in that imbalance.
  • The 6% supply increase is manageable but real. Adding approximately 116 million AERO via VELO conversion is not large dilution, but emissions from both legacy platforms stop at launch — removing a persistent seller while the new supply arrives in a single step.
  • Exchange friction creates short-term risk. Binance's November 3 cut-off and the unconfirmed Coinbase migration window mean holders who delay the upgrade could find themselves unable to move assets at the moment the market is most active around the new token.

What to expect

  • Tomorrow at 7:00 PM CT, the switch goes live and legacy emissions on both Aerodrome and Velodrome stop. The immediate test is whether upgrade portals handle conversion volume without congestion and whether the DNS-hijack playbook from November 2025 is exploited again — fake migration sites are a documented risk for this protocol.
  • Within the first week, TVL on the three zero-chain deployments will be the first measurable signal of whether Aero's cross-chain pitch is drawing fresh liquidity or just repositioning existing Base capital.
  • By early November, the Binance timeline will test how efficiently the market reprices old AERO versus new AERO; any spread between the two on secondary markets before the November 3 cut-off would likely compress sharply as the deadline approaches.
  • The medium-term case rests on whether Aero's real-time allocation model and 100% revenue-to-stakers structure attract liquidity providers that weekly voting cycles could not. If new-chain TVL grows and fee run-rate approaches the annualized $138 million implied by the last seven days, the current roughly six-times-fees multiple likely holds. If the new chains stay near zero, that multiple is the ceiling, not the floor.
