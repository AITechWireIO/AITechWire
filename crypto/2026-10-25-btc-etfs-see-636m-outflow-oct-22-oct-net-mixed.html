BTC ETFs see $636M outflow Oct 22, Oct net mixed

Volatility highlights sensitivity to macro signals ahead of Fed meeting.

AI TechWire · October 25, 2026 · 4 min read
Photo by Kanchanara / Unsplash

A single day of selling on October 22 erased nearly 60% of three sessions of Bitcoin ETF inflows and flipped the month negative — and prices barely flinched. That disconnect between fund flows and spot price is the real story: it tells you the marginal seller right now is institutional, not retail, and the Federal Reserve's October 27–28 meeting is the pin everyone is watching.

What Happened

U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs shed $635.6 million in net outflows on October 22, the largest single-day exit of the month, according to Farside Investors data. The move topped the previous monthly peak of $484.9 million recorded on October 7 and unwound almost 60% of the $1.0747 billion that had flowed in across the prior three sessions — $526.7 million on October 19, $23.2 million on October 20, and $524.8 million on October 21.

BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) and Fidelity's Wise Origin Bitcoin Fund (FBTC) led the retreat, posting $254.1 million and $217.5 million in outflows respectively. Together they accounted for roughly 74% of the day's total exit. ARK 21Shares Bitcoin ETF (ARKB) added another $109.4 million in outflows. Grayscale's GBTC contributed $29.5 million and Bitwise's BITB $27.4 million. The sole bright spot was Morgan Stanley's MSBT, which recorded a modest $2.3 million inflow.

The daily rout left October net flows in the red by roughly $176 million. Cumulative net inflows since the funds launched slipped to $57.8 billion, with total net assets sitting at $100.2 billion.

Crypto's broader cohort followed. Ethereum ETFs lost $110.6 million on the day, pushing their October losses to approximately $1.18 billion. Solana ETFs shed $8.9 million, bringing their monthly outflow to about $68 million. Yet spot prices were unmoved. Bitcoin traded near $81,460 on Friday, flat over 24 hours and down just 0.4% on the week. Ethereum dipped 0.2% to roughly $2,400. Solana slipped 0.3% to around $104. Total crypto market cap held at about $2.85 trillion.

Why It Matters: The Macro Override

The selling coincided with a broader risk-off session: oil climbed above $111 a barrel and AI stocks slid. Traders positioned ahead of the Federal Reserve's October 27–28 meeting, where a rate hold is widely expected. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, who took the role in May 2026 after Jerome Powell's term ended, has offered no signals of imminent easing, and that ambiguity is doing real work in institutional positioning.

The price resilience is the counterintuitive data point. When $635.6 million leaves ETF wrappers without moving spot, one of two things is happening: either offsetting demand is absorbing the sell pressure elsewhere, or ETF outflows are increasingly a poor proxy for aggregate conviction. In this case, the 30-day picture is more instructive — Bitcoin is down 4.8% over that window, Ethereum 11.4%, Solana 10.4% — suggesting the drift is real, just slow.

Trading volume also fell 20% to $67.1 billion on the day, reinforcing a picture of cautious, deliberate repositioning rather than panic selling. Institutional participants appear to be trimming exposure ahead of the Fed, not exiting a thesis.

"Over the long run, I expect ETFs to be a larger source of demand for bitcoin, with perpetual preferred offerings acting as a second tailwind addressing a different type of investor."

- Ryan Rasmussen, Head of Research, Bitwise

The Structural Bet Running Alongside

ETF flows don't exist in isolation. Corporate treasury buyers — specifically Strategy (MSTR) and Strive (ASST) — have quietly built a parallel demand channel through perpetual preferred stock issuances. Since their introduction roughly 20 months ago, those securities have financed purchases of approximately 136,000 bitcoin through October 5, according to Fidelity research analyst Zack Wainwright. That's more than 0.5% of bitcoin's maximum supply absorbed through a single funding mechanism.

Strategy, the largest corporate bitcoin holder, has five classes of preferred shares outstanding. Strive, the fourth-largest corporate holder, runs a similar playbook. Fidelity puts the bitcoin-focused preferred market at roughly $12 billion against a global fixed-income market of $150 trillion — a spread that implies significant room for growth if the model holds.

But the model has a structural tension built into it. As shares in circulation grow, so do dividend obligations. James Butterfill, CoinShares' head of research, noted that STRC alone raised $7.5 billion during 2026 through late July — carrying a variable dividend currently around 12%. That recurring cost competes directly with the ability to buy more bitcoin. Alex Blume, founder and CEO of institutional bitcoin asset manager Two Prime, pointed to STRC briefly falling into the $70 range in late June as evidence of the volatility embedded in these instruments.

Source: Mark Palmer, StoneX - "Perpetual preferreds have brought bitcoin to income investors, a group that ETFs never reached." CoinDesk

Palmer also flagged the reflexivity risk: continued preferred-funded bitcoin buying depends on those securities trading at or near par. Below par, the entire flywheel — issue preferred, buy bitcoin, support price, attract new issuance — risks stalling. The Strategy preferred currently trades around $99.40, close enough to par to keep the engine running, but not with much margin for error if the Fed meeting introduces fresh volatility.

Resolution: Two Demand Channels, One Pressure Point

The October 22 outflow is a stress test of a market now supported by two distinct institutional demand structures: spot ETFs capturing directional investors and perpetual preferred stock reaching yield-seeking capital that would never buy bitcoin directly. Neither channel is broken. But both are sensitive to the same macro variable — Fed policy — arriving in days.

If Warsh signals any tightening bias or holds rates with hawkish language, ETF outflows could accelerate and preferred valuations could slip toward the par threshold that constrains corporate treasury buying. If the Fed holds with a neutral tone, the rebound could be sharp given how little spot prices moved on $635.6 million in selling pressure.

What It Means

  • IBIT and FBTC drove 74% of Thursday's outflow — when the two largest funds move together, the signal is institutional repositioning, not retail sentiment; watch their daily flows as the cleaner Fed-sensitivity indicator.
  • Corporate treasury buyers like Strategy and Strive have absorbed over 136,000 BTC through preferred issuance, but dividend obligations and the requirement to trade near par mean that channel tightens exactly when markets do.
  • The October 27–28 Fed meeting under Chair Kevin Warsh is the near-term binary: a hawkish hold likely pressures both ETF inflows and preferred valuations simultaneously, compressing bitcoin's two largest institutional demand sources at once.
  • Price resilience despite $635.6 million in outflows suggests latent demand is absorbing supply — but the 30-day drawdowns across BTC, ETH and SOL confirm the drift is real, and further macro shocks would test whether that absorption holds.
