Bitcoin ETFs See $1.8B Weekly Outflows

US spot BTC ETFs recorded ~$1.79B net outflows for Nov 16-20 week, with IBIT leading redemptions; some rebound inflows via BlackRock. Signals weakening institutional demand amid rate hike fears and price weakness.

AI TechWire · November 23, 2026 · 4 min read
Photo by André François McKenzie / Unsplash

Bitcoin's spot ETF complex just posted its third-largest weekly outflow since the funds launched in January 2024 — and the damage is concentrated in the one fund that was supposed to prove institutional crypto adoption had arrived for good. BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust absorbed roughly three-quarters of last week's redemptions, turning what was once the market's proof-of-concept into its most visible pressure point.

What happened

US spot Bitcoin ETFs bled $1.79 billion in net outflows during the week ended November 20, according to SoSoValue data. That ranks as the third-largest weekly withdrawal since trading began, behind only the $2.61 billion exodus — the all-time single-week record — and a comparable period ended in late June.

The pain was not spread evenly. BlackRock's IBIT accounted for $1.37 billion, or roughly 76% of the total. The fund set consecutive single-day outflow records on Thursday and Friday: $541.1 million and $544.7 million respectively, surpassing the prior daily high of $528.3 million set on January 30. Those two days alone accounted for approximately $1.09 billion, nearly two-thirds of the week's entire industry-wide drain.

Zoom out and the picture is worse. Investors have now pulled a net $4.34 billion over four consecutive weeks, pushing year-to-date net outflows to roughly $3.8 billion. What started as episodic profit-taking has hardened into a sustained withdrawal trend that is difficult to dismiss as noise.

"IBIT accounted for ~$1.37 billion — about 76% of last week's total net outflows, setting new single-day outflow highs in a two-day span."

— SoSoValue data, via KuCoin News

The price backdrop matched the flow data in severity. Bitcoin traded near $64,000 on Thursday before sliding to $55,000 on Saturday morning, November 21 — its lowest level in more than two years — before recovering above $60,000 within hours. CoinGlass data showed roughly $2 billion in long liquidations over the prior 24 hours, followed by $230 million in short liquidations during the bounce. The violent reversal trapped traders on both sides within a single session.

Why it matters for institutional crypto exposure

The original bull case for spot Bitcoin ETFs was straightforward: regulated wrappers would bring in a steady wave of institutional and retail capital that would smooth volatility and provide durable price support. Last week's data complicates that thesis in two specific ways.

First, concentration risk is real. When a single product — IBIT — drives 76% of an industry-wide outflow week, the linkage between broader sentiment and that one fund becomes very tight. Any incremental shift in BlackRock's client base, or any macro catalyst that prompts institutional de-risking, can now move the needle on spot Bitcoin demand more directly than it could before IBIT existed.

Second, the year-to-date figure of $3.8 billion in net outflows reframes the narrative around ETF-driven adoption. These products have, in aggregate, seen more money leave than enter on a net basis in 2026. That does not mean the ETFs have failed — assets under management can still be substantial even amid net outflows if the funds were heavily subscribed earlier — but it does mean the argument that accompanied the January 2024 launch requires revision.

Hedge fund manager Bill Ackman's warning, flagged by Cointelegraph, that further Federal Reserve rate hikes could trigger a recession adds the macro layer. With Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's committee weighing another rate increase in December, risk assets broadly face a tighter-for-longer environment that is structurally unfavorable for non-yielding assets like Bitcoin.

The counter-view: one day of inflows does not a reversal make, but it matters

The story does not end entirely in the red. Reports indicate some rebound inflows arrived via BlackRock on November 23, suggesting that at least a portion of institutional capital views prices near $60,000 as an entry point rather than a level to flee. The Saturday bounce that wiped out $230 million in short positions also demonstrated that demand exists beneath the recent lows; bears who pressed the move below $55,000 were forced out quickly.

The more structurally interesting question is whether IBIT's consecutive daily outflow records represent a one-time institutional repositioning — pension funds and RIAs trimming risk ahead of year-end, say — or whether they signal something more fundamental about how large allocators now view Bitcoin inside a rising-rate, risk-off regime. A single day of inflows does not answer that question. The pace of IBIT's flows over the next two weeks will.

Altcoins offered a secondary data point. Ethereum bounced from near $1,500 to around $1,700; BNB recovered from $500 to $545; XRP moved from a test of $0.85 support back toward $0.92. The breadth of the recovery suggests the weekend's liquidation cascade cleared enough leveraged longs to stabilize the broader market temporarily — but stabilization after a liquidation flush is not the same as a resumption of inflows into regulated ETF products.

What to watch next

The open question is whether $55,000 marked a durable floor or merely a pause. Bitcoin's ability to hold above $60,000 through the coming business week is the near-term test. Beyond price, the more consequential signal will come from daily ETF flow data: if IBIT's outflow pace decelerates meaningfully or flips to consistent net inflows, it would suggest the institutional selling was exhaustion-driven rather than a structural exit. If outflows persist at last week's pace, year-to-date figures will deepen further and the AUM erosion will begin to attract attention from allocators who benchmark against peers.

What it means

  • BlackRock bears the most reputational weight: IBIT's dominance of outflows means its flow data is now the single most-watched institutional sentiment gauge in crypto — any continued redemption pressure likely drags spot price directly.
  • Four consecutive outflow weeks signal a regime shift, not an episode: With $4.34 billion withdrawn over a month and year-to-date net outflows at $3.8 billion, the narrative needs to be retired until sustained inflows return.
  • Rate-hike risk is the macro wildcard: Bill Ackman's recession warning tied to a potential December Fed move suggests the macro headwind for Bitcoin ETF demand could intensify before it eases.
  • Watch the $60,000 level and daily IBIT flows: A failure to hold $60,000 through next week would test whether November 21's capitulation was a durable floor; IBIT inflow data on a day-by-day basis is the clearest leading indicator of whether institutional demand is rebuilding.
