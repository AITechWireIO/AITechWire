Aave v4 Avalanche Deposits Surge Past $225M

Deposits up 295% since late Oct with loans >$100M on Aave's first non-Eth expansion. Signals growing DeFi activity on alt L1s.

AI TechWire · November 15, 2026 · 4 min read
Photo by Shubham Dhage / Unsplash

Aave v4 on Avalanche Just Hit $225M in Deposits — and the Market Hasn't Noticed Yet

Capital is quietly rotating into Avalanche's DeFi ecosystem even as the broader crypto market stumbles. Aave v4 deposits on Avalanche have surged to $225 million — a 295% jump in roughly two weeks — and the price of AVAX barely flinched. That divergence is the most important signal in the data.

What Happened

When Aave v4 launched its Avalanche markets on July 15, it marked the protocol's first expansion beyond Ethereum. The deployment uses a Hub-and-Spoke architecture, allowing markets with comparable risk profiles to draw on liquidity held in a shared Hub, subject to preset limits. It was a deliberate, structured bet on Avalanche as a credible DeFi venue — not a speculative sprint.

The numbers since late October confirm the bet is paying off on the liquidity side. Total deposits climbed from roughly $57 million to $225 million in approximately two weeks, a 295% move. At the same time, active loans on Aave v4's Avalanche deployment crossed $100 million, a figure that matters because it separates passive deposits from genuine protocol utility. Users aren't just parking capital — they're borrowing against it, which means the lending markets are functioning as designed.

Aave v4's total deposits across all chains also crossed $1.75 billion, an all-time high for the protocol. Avalanche's contribution to that record is no longer negligible.

"Active loans for Aave v4 on Avalanche topped the $100 million mark. This implied that both liquidity and utility were growing on the chain."

- AMBCrypto

Why It Matters

The DeFi thesis on alternative Layer-1 blockchains has had a rough few years. Ethereum's dominance in total value locked has been treated as near-permanent, and many rival chains saw their DeFi ecosystems deflate after the 2021–2022 cycle. Aave's decision to make Avalanche its first non-Ethereum v4 deployment was itself a statement. The deposit surge validates that decision with on-chain evidence.

The Hub-and-Spoke design is worth understanding here because it's not just an architectural detail — it's a capital efficiency argument. By allowing multiple markets to share a liquidity hub rather than siloing funds, Aave v4 can serve borrowers more effectively without requiring each market to independently bootstrap deep reserves. That design choice is a meaningful reason why $225 million in deposits can support $100 million in active loans at this stage of the deployment's life.

For Ava Labs, the numbers represent external validation at a moment when the organization is in the middle of a leadership transition. Charley Cooper stepped into the President role, with Lydia Chiu confirmed as permanent CFO at the same time. Founder and CEO Emin Gün Sirer remains at the helm. A surging DeFi metric like this gives the new leadership team a tangible win to point to as they execute their strategy.

Meanwhile, AVAX One — a Nasdaq-listed treasury vehicle focused on Avalanche tokens and trading under the ticker AVX — reported $49.90 million in Q3 net income, driven by $55.10 million in unrealized digital asset gains. Revenue came in at $2.40 million, up more than four times year-over-year compared to the year-ago period in Q3 2025. The firm eliminated over $14.90 million in convertible debt during the quarter, bringing its balance to zero. It now holds 14.17 million AVAX, with 99.7% of that amount staked at an annualized rate of 5.85%. The treasury also repurchased 384,000 shares in Q3 and an additional 701,000 shares in October. Institutional capital is accumulating at the protocol level, and it's accumulating aggressively.

The Counter-View: On-Chain Growth, Off-Chain Indifference

None of this has moved AVAX's price in any meaningful way. At the time the deposit figures were reported, AVAX was testing support between $8 and $9, with the MACD histogram flashing bearish momentum and the next demand level sitting around $7. The market has looked at $225 million in DeFi deposits, $100 million in active loans, record protocol-wide totals, and a profitable institutional treasury — and shrugged.

This is not a minor inconvenience for the bull case. It's the central tension. DeFi metrics and token price have historically correlated, at least directionally. When they diverge this sharply, one of two things is usually true: either the market is wrong and price will catch up to fundamentals, or the DeFi metrics are being driven by activity that doesn't generate the kind of organic demand pressure that moves spot prices. Capital rotating in from yield-seekers looking for efficiency gains, rather than speculative buyers, can produce impressive TVL numbers without lifting the native token.

The open question is which dynamic is dominant here. The loan-to-deposit ratio — with $100 million in active loans against $225 million in deposits — suggests real utility rather than purely mercenary yield farming. But until that utility translates into fee revenue that benefits AVAX holders directly, or until broader market sentiment turns, the price may remain disconnected from the protocol's operational momentum.

Resolution: Capital Is Moving Before Price Does

What the Aave v4 Avalanche data captures is a rotation that typically precedes broader recognition. Institutional and sophisticated DeFi capital tends to move first, into infrastructure that works, before retail price action follows. The $225 million deposit figure, the $100 million loan milestone, and the AVAX One treasury's aggressive accumulation are all consistent with a setup phase — not a completed trade.

Aave Labs, led by founder Stani Kulechov, built a protocol that now has its deepest non-Ethereum footprint on Avalanche. That's a durable structural advantage for the chain, whatever the next few weeks of price action look like.

What It Means

  • Aave v4's Avalanche deployment is now a material contributor to the protocol's all-time high total deposits of $1.75 billion — this likely means more protocol-level attention and resources will continue flowing to the chain.
  • The $100 million active loan figure separates Avalanche from DeFi chains that attract deposits without generating real borrowing demand; watch this number for signs of sustained utility or early plateau.
  • AVAX One's zero-debt balance sheet and 14.17 million staked tokens represent concentrated institutional exposure — if price recovers, the upside capture is significant; if support at $8 breaks toward $7, that same concentration becomes a risk.
  • The price-activity divergence is the clearest near-term signal to monitor: a close above the recent range on strong volume would confirm the on-chain data is finally feeding through to market demand.
