Anthropic Leads AI Model Benchmarks

Polymarket odds show Anthropic at 98.6% chance of best AI model end of September per LiveBench coding metrics. Matters for AI investors tracking model supremacy and valuation implications.

AI TechWire · September 26, 2026 · 5 min read
Photo by Brecht Corbeel / Unsplash

Anthropic just handed AI investors a landmark data point. On September 22, 2026, the company released Claude Opus 5.5 — and independent benchmarker Artificial Analysis promptly ranked it first on its Intelligence Index with a score of 58, five points clear of GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1, both sitting at 53. In a market where model supremacy directly shapes valuation narratives, that gap is not a footnote. It is the headline.

The Numbers That Moved the Needle

The Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3 combines ten evaluations: AA-Briefcase, GDPval-AA v2, AutomationBench-AA, Terminal-Bench 4.0, SciCode, Humanity's Last Exam, GDP.pdf, CritPt, AA-Omniscience, and AA-LCR v1.1. Tested at its maximum effort setting with fallback enabled, Claude Opus 5.5 scored 58 — what Artificial Analysis called "the highest score we have measured by several points." The previous Claude Opus 5 had scored 51. That is a seven-point single-generation jump, and it matters.

Opus 5.5 leads on six of the ten evaluations outright, including Humanity's Last Exam at 61.4%, SciCode at 66.9%, GDPval-AA, AA-Briefcase, AA-Omniscience, and AutomationBench-AA. On Terminal-Bench 4.0, it ties GPT-6 Astra at 59.6%. The gap between Anthropic's flagship and the best open-weights model — Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.6-Pro at 46 — now stands at 12 points. For anyone tracking proprietary AI moats, that spread is the number to watch.

The largest single margin comes on AA-Briefcase, Artificial Analysis's agentic knowledge-work benchmark. There, Opus 5.5 at maximum effort reaches 1,822 Elo against 1,678 for Claude Fable 5.1. On GDPval-AA v2.1, it posts 1,846 Elo. These are not incremental improvements. They represent Anthropic pulling meaningfully ahead on the class of complex, long-horizon tasks that enterprise buyers — and enterprise valuations — are built around.

Where OpenAI Still Holds Ground

The picture is not entirely one-sided, and intellectual honesty demands noting where GPT-6 Astra pushes back. According to Anthropic's own benchmark disclosures, Astra leads on AutomationBench — a Zapier-run business workflows test — and on Terminal-Bench-Science, an agentic scientific research evaluation where Astra scores 64.6% against Opus 5.5's 58.7%. Anthropic itself flagged that its own safeguards intervened during several evaluations, quietly rerouting cybersecurity and biology-adjacent tasks to the older Opus 4.8 model — which, by its own admission, likely dragged its scores down on those specific tests.

There is also the token-efficiency question. Opus 5.5 at maximum effort uses approximately 119,000 output tokens per Intelligence Index task, compared to roughly 73,000 for Opus 5 and just 27,000 for GPT-6 Astra. Anthropic's answer is the price cut: because input and output pricing dropped to $4 and $20 per million tokens respectively — down from $5 and $25 for Opus 5 — and cache reads fell 60% to $0.20 per million tokens, Artificial Analysis found that cost per task stays level with Opus 5 despite the 1.6x token increase. Four of Opus 5.5's five effort levels sit on the intelligence-versus-cost Pareto frontier.

Real-World Performance Backs the Benchmarks

Benchmark critics always ask whether the numbers translate. In this case, the evidence is pointed. Anthropic cites one tester who used Opus 5.5 to audit and fix a 200,000-line codebase in under three hours — a task that took over 20 hours with Opus 5. Another ran a 680,000-line code migration in under a day. In an internal test rewriting the load-balancing software HAProxy from C into Rust, Opus 5.5 finished in 9.5 hours against Fable 5.1's 12, at just under half the cost.

Third-party validation tightens the case. Deloitte Consulting's CIO Carl Bennett said Opus 5.5 caught more known bugs in code review at its lowest effort setting than Opus 5 did at its highest. GitHub's chief product officer Mario Rodriguez said the model used among the fewest tokens and steps of any model his team tested, solving more terminal tasks in VS Code in under half the steps Opus 5 needed. These are not synthetic lab results. They are production-grade signals from organizations that move slowly and carefully when endorsing vendor claims.

What This Means for the Competitive Map

The release lands against a specific backdrop: it is Anthropic's first major model drop since CEO Dario Amodei's public essay calling on the industry to slow frontier AI development. The company also deployed Opus 5.5 with substantive safeguards — most cybersecurity tasks are rerouted to the older Opus 4.8, and deeper biology work requires vetted access through Anthropic's Life Sciences Verification Program. External evaluators including METR tested the model pre-launch.

That combination — a benchmark-leading model released under a safety-forward posture — is precisely the brand architecture Anthropic has been building toward. A five-point lead on the most comprehensive independent index available, paired with a 20% price cut and a 30%-faster generation speed, is not a soft launch. It is a statement of competitive positioning.

OpenAI is not finished. Astra still leads on specific agentic science tasks, and the token-efficiency gap is real. But as of September 22, 2026, the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index says Anthropic holds the top spot — and in the AI valuation race, holding the top spot, even temporarily, has historically been worth more than analysts price in before it happens.

Anthropic has the lead. The question now is how long it can hold it — and what OpenAI's response looks like when it arrives.
