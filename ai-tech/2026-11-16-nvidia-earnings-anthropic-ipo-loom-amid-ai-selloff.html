Nvidia Earnings, Anthropic IPO Loom Amid AI Selloff

Nvidia reports Q3 results Tuesday after CEO Jensen Huang's resignation amid scandal; Anthropic set to price its massive IPO Wednesday. Markets price in volatility as AI trade tests resilience ahead of Fed minutes and rate hike bets.

AI TechWire · November 16, 2026 · 4 min read
Photo by Igor Omilaev / Unsplash

Nvidia Earnings and Anthropic IPO Are the AI Trade's Last Line of Defense

The AI bull market that powered four years of record equity gains is now being stress-tested in a single week, and the results will tell investors whether this is a healthy correction or the beginning of something worse. Nvidia reports Tuesday, Anthropic prices Wednesday, and by Thursday morning the market will have its answer.

The Selloff in Numbers

The damage to Nvidia is not subtle. Reuters confirmed by November 13 that shares had fallen roughly 30% from their October 6 high, erasing almost $2 trillion in market value in a little over a month. A two-session plunge before a brief bounce wiped out about $430 billion alone. The stock clawed back 2.4% on November 11, then gave it back, closing the following week down about 7%. By Friday, LSEG data showed Nvidia's market value had fallen to $3.9 trillion from approximately $5.8 trillion in early October.

That is not noise. For a company that at its peak represented nearly 8% of the S&P 500, a 30% drawdown is an index-level event. Investors who never bought a single share of Nvidia are sitting on losses they did not choose.

Tech stocks in Asia reflected the same anxiety overnight Monday, with Taiwanese equities dropping nearly 5% and South Korean markets off 3%. ASML, Infineon, and Siemens were the biggest drags on Europe's STOXX 600. The selling is not confined to one ticker or one timezone.

Three Catalysts Colliding at Once

What makes this week unusually treacherous is the convergence of independent risk events, each of which would be significant on its own.

First, Nvidia reports Q3 FY2027 earnings after the close on Tuesday, November 17. Consensus expects substantial revenue growth and earnings of $2.47 per share. Fourth-quarter guidance is expected to show strong revenue growth and earnings growth to $2.75 per share. The numbers are enormous, but the market's standard for Nvidia has never been "meet expectations." Investors expect the company to overwhelm them. Anything short of a blowout risks being read as confirmation that the AI spending cycle is decelerating.

Second, the company reporting those numbers is operating without the CEO who built it. Jensen Huang resigned as President, CEO, and board member effective November 12, following the emergence of a scandal involving an extramarital affair with a woman US authorities suspect of having links to Chinese intelligence. CFO Colette Kress was appointed Interim CEO while continuing in her role as CFO. Harvey C. Jones, a board member since 1993, was named Chair. Nvidia's board told CNBC on Friday it has begun the process of selecting a search firm to find a permanent CEO. The leadership vacuum is expected to generate as much investor attention as the revenue line itself.

Third, Anthropic is set to price its Nasdaq IPO on Wednesday, November 18, with trading beginning Thursday under the ticker ANT. The implied market valuation sits between $1.3 trillion and $1.45 trillion. A $26 billion private placement already locked in Google, Nvidia, AMD, Broadcom, Micron, Microsoft, and TSMC as non-voting shareholders at the IPO price. Anthropic reported a net loss exceeding $104 billion in the nine months through September 2026, though the company has noted that most of that figure reflects non-cash accounting adjustments to financing instruments rather than cash burn.

Layered on top of all of this: the Fed's October meeting minutes drop Wednesday, and markets are watching for any signal that policymakers are open to an outsized 50-basis-point rate hike at the December meeting. Rate sensitivity is not Nvidia's primary story, but it is the ambient pressure that makes every growth valuation harder to defend.

"I think both events have the potential to be positive catalysts for the AI trade, which has otherwise been hitting a lot of bumps in the road over the course of the last two weeks."

- Art Hogan, Chief Market Strategist, B Riley Wealth

The Counter-Case: This Week Could Stabilize Everything

The bear case is loud, but it is not the only story. CoreWeave — a hyperscaler closely tied to Nvidia's data center business — moved up 16% from an 18-month low after its own earnings, a data point that suggests AI infrastructure demand has not evaporated. If Nvidia's results show that hyperscalers are still ordering chips at scale, the narrative flips fast.

Anthropic's IPO carries the same dual potential. At a valuation between $1.3 trillion and $1.45 trillion, the deal is a referendum on whether institutional capital still believes in AI at scale. The $26 billion private placement, with every major name in tech already on the cap table, is designed to send exactly that signal. A clean pricing and a strong first-day open on Thursday would give AI bulls their most tangible piece of evidence in weeks that the trade is not broken, merely bruised.

Source: Art Hogan, B Riley Wealth - "We're still in early innings of this game, and the impact of artificial intelligence is going to be massive." link

The open question is not whether Nvidia's business is healthy — the consensus numbers suggest it is — but whether a company in leadership transition can deliver the kind of confident forward guidance that markets need to reverse a 30% drawdown. Colette Kress is a credible operator. Whether she can play the role of AI economy's chief narrator, the part Jensen Huang owned for years, is something Tuesday's earnings call will begin to answer.

What It Means

  • Nvidia's earnings print is binary for the near term: a revenue beat with strong Q4 guidance likely stabilizes the broader AI trade; a miss or cautious outlook risks accelerating the rotation out of AI-exposed equities and ETFs.
  • The leadership transition is now a persistent discount on the stock — Kress's performance on the earnings call will either narrow or widen that discount heading into the CEO search.
  • Anthropic's IPO pricing Wednesday is the market's clearest real-time signal on institutional appetite for AI at scale; a clean deal at the top of the range would validate the AI investment thesis in a way no earnings report can.
  • Fed minutes Wednesday add a second variable: any hawkish signal on a 50-basis-point December hike compresses growth multiples across the sector and makes the recovery harder, regardless of what Nvidia reports.
