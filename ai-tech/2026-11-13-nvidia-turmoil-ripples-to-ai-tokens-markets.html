NVIDIA Turmoil Ripples to AI Tokens & Markets

Huang exit fuels volatility in AI infrastructure plays and tokens like TAO/NEAR; prediction markets price Huang's new ventures and NVIDIA valuation scenarios. Compounds broader market jitters.

NVIDIA's Leadership Void Sends Shockwaves From Chip Stocks to Crypto Markets

Jensen Huang's abrupt resignation has done something few single events can: it moved Bitcoin. The NVIDIA co-founder's exit, announced November 12, is not just a corporate governance story — it is a demand shock for the entire AI infrastructure stack, and the first tremors landed in leveraged crypto markets within hours.

What Happened: One Announcement, Two Market Selloffs

The sequence is now documented. On Thursday afternoon, NVIDIA confirmed that Huang had resigned as President, CEO, and Board Member after more than 33 years leading the company. CFO Colette Kress was named Interim CEO — retaining her CFO responsibilities — while longtime director Harvey C. Jones was appointed the company's first-ever Chair of the Board. A permanent CEO search, covering both internal and external candidates, is underway.

Within hours of the announcement, crypto markets cracked. According to CoinGlass data, the first burst of liquidations hit at 14:00 UTC Thursday, precisely as the NVIDIA news broke. Bitcoin fell through $70,000 overnight, triggering a second liquidation wave at 03:00 UTC Friday when it breached $69,000. By Friday, BTC had touched a low of $67,568 on Binance — its weakest level since August 19 — and was trading at $68,772, down 6.7% in 24 hours and 9.1% on the week. Ether lost 5.5% and Solana 3.4% on the day. Crypto markets shed roughly $150 billion in 48 hours.

Liquidations totaled approximately $1.4 billion over 24 hours, with close to 90% coming from long positions. On OKX alone, Bitcoin longs absorbed $76.7 million in forced closures. Longs took about 11 times more damage than shorts — a ratio that reflects just how crowded the bullish trade had become heading into the week.

Why It Matters: The Huang Premium, Repriced

NVIDIA is not simply a chip company — under Huang, it became the central nervous system of the global AI buildout. The company's GPUs underpin the training and deployment of large language models, and Huang personally drove the company's most consequential strategic moves: the $6.9 billion acquisition of Israeli chipmaker Mellanox in 2019, the establishment of NVIDIA's largest R&D facility outside the U.S. in Israel, and plans for a multibillion-dollar campus in northern Israel. The company employs more than 6,000 people there.

The question now is whether NVIDIA's strategic momentum survives the man who built it. Investors are repricing that uncertainty in real time.

"Slower decisions at Nvidia could give competitors an opening, but if that uncertainty also slows AI spending, they could end up with a bigger share of a smaller opportunity."

- Yanai Oron, General Partner, Vertex Ventures Israel

That framing captures the trap facing AI infrastructure plays broadly. If NVIDIA's pace of investment slows — Huang's cash-deployment machine had been funding data center guarantees worth billions and backing next-generation chip development — the entire ecosystem that depended on that capital velocity feels the drag. That includes AI-adjacent tokens and decentralized compute projects that have traded, in part, on the assumption that NVIDIA-fueled AI demand would keep expanding.

The Wall Street Journal, citing an anonymous source, added another layer: Huang reportedly described Kress as "a deer in the headlights" during a summer presentation and said she was someone who "couldn't help him steer strategy or decision-making." The report also noted that Kress had scaled back NVIDIA's commitment to OpenAI's Ohio data center project — the company ultimately committed $105 billion rather than the $250 billion initially discussed. Whether that represents prudent risk management or a contraction of NVIDIA's ambition is the central debate now playing out in markets.

The Counter-Case: Ecosystem Gravity Is Real

The strongest pushback to the bear case comes from investors closest to NVIDIA's infrastructure. Even those in Israel — where anxiety about leadership continuity runs highest — are not calling for a collapse in AI compute demand.

Source: TechCrunch / Anna Heim - Adi Levanon, Selah Ventures: "[Huang's] departure doesn't dismantle Nvidia's technology or ecosystem, but it could give competitors more room to shape what comes next." link

The GPU moat — built on CUDA, developer ecosystems, and years of hardware iteration — does not evaporate because the founder left. Enterprise AI deployments do not pause pending CEO confirmation. The demand that NVIDIA's chips serve is structural, not personal. What changes is the competitive surface area: challengers building AI-specific inference chips and alternative compute software now have a credibility window they did not have six months ago. As Levanon put it, "these challengers now have a window to prove a different approach delivers better economics; a leadership change alone won't win them the business."

The sentiment data from crypto markets actually supports this reading in a counterintuitive way. The Fear & Greed Index dropped to 20 — Extreme Fear — on Friday, its lowest reading since July. Historical analysis of every Extreme Fear episode since 2018 shows that Bitcoin was higher 90 days later in 7 of 16 cases. The median further decline was 23.2%, but the data also confirms that forced liquidation cascades of this scale tend to flush out the weakest hands rather than signal structural demand destruction.

What to Watch: Succession, Spending, and the New Competitive Map

The open question is not whether NVIDIA survives — it will. The question is what version of NVIDIA emerges from this search. Names circulating include Apple's Johny Srouji and NVIDIA's Michael Kagan, though the list remains tentative. Kress, meanwhile, carries both the CFO role and the interim CEO burden into what may be a months-long transition — managing a company under congressional scrutiny, a reported Chinese espionage investigation, and a press cycle that is not done with the Huang scandal.

For AI compute demand and the tokens and equities that trade on it, the volatility is a rotation signal as much as a risk signal. Capital that had priced in NVIDIA's unchecked dominance is now hedging. Capital looking for the next infrastructure layer is hunting for entries.

What It Means

  • NVIDIA's strategic pace — data center financing, next-gen chip investment, M&A — is likely to slow under an interim CEO who has already shown preference for caution; this benefits AI compute challengers seeking enterprise footholds.
  • The crypto liquidation cascade was NVIDIA-triggered but crypto-structural: more than 3 long positions remain open for every short on OKX, meaning forced selling pressure is not fully exhausted.
  • Israel's tech ecosystem, which depends heavily on NVIDIA's local commitments, faces real uncertainty about whether a permanent successor maintains Huang's strategic ties — watch the Mellanox campus and R&D investment decisions as early signals.
  • The permanent CEO search is the single most important variable in NVIDIA's near-term valuation; internal candidates with product depth would likely be read as continuity, while an external hire signals a governance reset the market has not yet priced.
