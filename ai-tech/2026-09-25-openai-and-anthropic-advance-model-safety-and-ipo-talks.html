OpenAI and Anthropic Advance Model Safety and IPO Talks

Frontier labs continue internal safety testing and valuation races, with Anthropic eyeing high targets and OpenAI deferring listings. Focus remains on capability thresholds and compute scaling.

AI TechWire · September 25, 2026 · 5 min read
Photo by Google DeepMind / Unsplash

The AGI era, according to OpenAI, has already begun. While Wall Street debates IPO timelines and safety regulators sharpen their pencils, frontier labs are moving faster than the conversation around them — launching models they're calling civilization-altering and watching the capital markets scramble to keep up. The race isn't slowing down. If anything, this week proved it's accelerating.

GPT-6 Astra: OpenAI Plants Its AGI Flag

OpenAI didn't whisper its latest milestone — it declared it. The company this week released GPT-6 Astra, a frontier model it says likely marks the onset of artificial general intelligence, defined by the company as "highly autonomous systems that outperform humans at most economically valuable work."

The framing was deliberate and pointed. In a closed press briefing, OpenAI co-founder and president Greg Brockman ended the session with a line that will echo through boardrooms and regulatory hearings alike.

"Welcome to the AGI era."

- Greg Brockman, Co-founder and President, OpenAI

Astra's headline capability isn't just raw reasoning power — it's computer use. OpenAI is positioning the model as "the world's best computer use model," one that can navigate browsers, fill out forms, update CRM records, manipulate spreadsheets, analyze scientific data in Python notebooks, work in Power BI, and even operate engineering applications like KiCad and FreeCAD. All from voice input alone.

The practical implication is significant. For years, enterprises have had to build expensive API integrations and custom connectors to stitch AI into their workflows. Brockman argued that Astra's computer-use capabilities could begin bypassing that infrastructure entirely.

Source: Greg Brockman, OpenAI - "We've been bottlenecked over this gigantic era by people writing connectors and very painstakingly building these connections into all these tools that people can already use." link

The benchmarks back up the ambition. On an offline subset of OSWorld 2.0, Astra scored 72.6% while completing tasks in roughly 40 minutes each — compared to its predecessor GPT-5.6 Sol's score of 65.7% at approximately 75 minutes per task. That's not just a better model. That's roughly a 47% improvement in speed alongside a meaningful accuracy jump.

Astra is rolling out first to enterprise customers through OpenAI's gated access program, Daybreak, before expanding to ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Business, and Enterprise customers, as well as through the OpenAI API and cloud platforms including AWS Bedrock and Microsoft Azure.

Capital Follows Conviction: Venture Bets on the AI Stack

If OpenAI's launch is the headline, the money moving beneath the surface is the subtext. Frontier AI ambitions require frontier capital, and investors are positioning accordingly — not just in the U.S., but globally.

Lightspeed Venture Partners is targeting $250 million for a new early-stage fund — Lightspeed India Partners V — with a sharp focus on AI across India and Southeast Asia. The fund has already secured commitments for 80% of its target, according to a letter sent to investors and seen by TechCrunch.

Lightspeed is already a major investor in Anthropic, xAI, and Databricks, giving the firm direct exposure to the frontier model race. Its India thesis is equally bullish: the investor letter anticipates AI creating more value in India than the internet did — a striking claim for a market that has yet to produce a globally competitive frontier model developer.

The new fund is half the size of its $500 million predecessor raised in 2022, a deliberate choice. Lightspeed told investors the smaller size allows it to focus on individual deals rather than fund scale — and to return to market sooner. The firm manages more than $65 billion in assets globally and raised $9 billion across several new funds last December alone, the largest fundraising haul in its history.

Lightspeed also plans to begin deploying from the new fund within two months, with an investment period of roughly two and a half years. The urgency is deliberate. In AI, time is the scarcest asset.

What the AGI Declaration Means for Regulators and the Market

OpenAI calling GPT-6 Astra the likely dawn of AGI is not a neutral technical statement. It's a legal, regulatory, and commercial provocation — one with real consequences.

Regulators in Washington, Brussels, and beyond have been watching frontier labs closely, but the pace of capability development has consistently outrun policy responses. A company publicly declaring it has crossed the AGI threshold — however that threshold is defined — changes the political calculus. Safety testing frameworks, compute governance, and model access controls all become more urgent.

For investors, the signal cuts differently. If AGI-class systems can autonomously operate software, conduct research, and execute multi-step enterprise workflows without human handholding, the total addressable market for AI expands dramatically — and so does the pressure on every incumbent software vendor. Enterprise SaaS companies built on the assumption that humans will always click the buttons should be paying very close attention.

Lightspeed's aggressive capital deployment into AI — from Anthropic at the frontier to early-stage India startups at the application layer — reflects a bet that the entire stack is about to be repriced. That bet looks increasingly well-timed.

The AGI era, whether or not that label holds up to scrutiny, is now a declared market reality. The labs have made their move. Regulators, investors, and enterprises are all on the clock.
