Apple Advances Agentic Siri in iOS

Apple is pushing Siri toward agentic app control in upcoming iOS updates, with first-party actions and third-party support planned for 2027. This positions the assistant as a deeper AI interface amid agent hype.

AI TechWire · November 09, 2026 · 4 min read
Photo by Sumudu Mohottige / Unsplash

Apple Is Done Playing Catch-Up on AI Agents — and the Rest of the Industry Should Take Notice

Apple is moving to transform Siri from a reactive voice assistant into a genuine AI agent, and the pressure coming from inside the company makes clear this is no longer a roadmap aspiration — it's a competitive emergency. With CEO John Ternus pushing software chief Craig Federighi to accelerate the Siri roadmap, Apple is signaling that the era of incremental assistant upgrades is over.

What Happened

Bloomberg's Mark Gurman reported this week that Apple wants to make Siri "more like an agent as early as next year." The reporting lands at a revealing moment: Apple only just delivered its long-promised personalized Siri with iOS 27, a release that gave the assistant access to photos, emails, messages, and calendar data, and enabled on-screen awareness through Visual Intelligence. That was a genuine milestone — but the AI landscape didn't pause to applaud.

Two specific upgrades are now in the pipeline. The first, expected with iOS 27.3, gives Siri fine-grained control over Apple's own first-party apps — think rotating and cropping images in Photos, or organizing tabs in Safari. The iOS 27.3 beta released last week already opened third-party developer integration, meaning Siri could soon place a Grubhub order or book a dinner reservation through OpenTable. The larger agentic leap — persistent, goal-tracking behavior that doesn't require a user to initiate every step — is targeted for sometime next year.

Gurman also surfaced an internal process change worth watching: Apple has restructured its iOS 28 development cycle, extending the initial coding and exploration phase from four weeks to six, while compressing the integration window from four weeks to two. Engineers reportedly welcome the shift, saying it gives them more room to develop and test ideas before features get locked in. That kind of structural change — quiet, procedural, easy to overlook — often signals that leadership has decided the old pace simply isn't acceptable anymore.

"Apple ultimately wants to go in this direction, I'm told, and I believe the company could make Siri more like an agent as early as next year — not that it has much of a choice, given current consumer and market demands."

- Mark Gurman, Bloomberg Power On newsletter

Why It Matters

The competitive context here is not subtle. OpenAI's Dots and Meta's Muse are already operating as agentic systems — tracking goals, determining next steps, and completing tasks in the background without waiting for user prompts. These aren't prototypes; they are live products shaping consumer expectations right now. Apple, with over a billion active iPhone users, has the distribution advantage that no AI lab can match. If Siri becomes a credible agent, it becomes the default agent for the largest installed base of premium devices on earth.

What gives Apple a structural edge in this race isn't just scale — it's architecture. The company has spent years building frameworks like App Intents specifically to allow apps to expose their functionality to system-level orchestration. Agents built on that foundation don't need to resort to the kind of computer-use workarounds — essentially screen-scraping and simulated clicks — that make cloud-based agents expensive and unreliable. Apple's approach, if executed well, could deliver agentic behavior that is faster, cheaper to run, and more privacy-preserving than anything a pure-cloud competitor can offer today.

Ternus reportedly held a closed-door meeting with Federighi specifically to push Siri's development faster. That detail is telling. Apple's new CEO inherited a company that had already faced lawsuits over delayed AI promises tied to the iPhone 16, and he is clearly not content to let the gap with OpenAI and Meta persist. The internal frustration, now on the record, is itself a signal to the market.

The Counter-View: Apple Has Promised This Before

Skepticism is earned here. Apple's track record on AI timelines is not clean. The personal context and cross-app capabilities that now define iOS 27's Siri were first promised alongside the iPhone 16 and missed badly — badly enough to generate lawsuits and require a partnership with Google to fill the gap. The company announced what it couldn't ship, then scrambled. Saying that agentic Siri is coming "as early as next year" is not the same thing as shipping it.

The open question is whether Apple's new development process — longer coding cycles, more exploration time — represents genuine structural reform or is simply a rebalancing that looks good on paper. Agentic AI is also a harder technical and product problem than context-aware search. Keeping track of goals across sessions, knowing when to act and when to wait, handling failures gracefully — these are unsolved problems even for the labs that have been working on them longest. Apple is entering this space later and will need to execute faster than it has historically managed on AI.

Resolution: The Race Is Real, the Stakes Are High

What's changed is the mandate. Under Ternus, Apple is no longer treating AI as a feature set to be refined on its own schedule. The closed-door meeting with Federighi, the restructured development cycle, and the explicit reporting that Apple "has no choice" given market demands all point to an organization that has accepted urgency as a design constraint. That is a meaningfully different Apple than the one that let Siri stagnate for most of the last decade.

The first real test comes with iOS 27.3, where first-party app control will either demonstrate that Apple's agentic architecture is ready for prime time or reveal new gaps. Third-party integration arriving in spring would be the second checkpoint. The full agentic vision — persistent, goal-driven Siri — lands sometime after that, in what Apple is now actively developing as iOS 28.

What It Means

  • Apple holds a structural advantage: Its App Intents framework likely means agentic Siri could be less expensive and more reliable than cloud-dependent competitors — but only if Apple ships on time, which remains the company's most credible risk.
  • OpenAI and Meta face their most dangerous challenger yet: No AI lab can match Apple's installed base; a functional Siri agent would immediately become the highest-distribution AI agent product in the world.
  • iOS 27.3 is the near-term signal to watch: First-party app control is the first concrete deliverable on the agentic roadmap — its quality and scope will indicate whether Apple's accelerated timeline is credible.
  • Ternus's impatience likely means faster, riskier launches: The CEO's direct pressure on Federighi suggests Apple may be willing to ship earlier and iterate, a cultural shift that could cut both ways on quality and trust.
