Prediction Markets Hit Record October Volumes

Kalshi and Polymarket set all-time monthly volume records with Kalshi at $65B+ MTD. Driven by sports, elections, and macro events with open interest at highs.

AI TechWire · October 31, 2026 · 4 min read
Photo by Maxim Hopman / Unsplash

Prediction markets have crossed a threshold that changes how we talk about them. Kalshi and Polymarket US didn't just post strong October numbers — they set all-time monthly volume records with nearly a week still left in the month, signaling that these platforms have graduated from niche curiosity to a fixture of financial market infrastructure. The question now isn't whether prediction markets matter. It's how much they're about to reshape the pricing of political and macro risk.

The Numbers That Change the Conversation

Kalshi recorded approximately $65 billion in October notional trading volume, roughly 10% above its September full-month record — itself a previous high-water mark. Polymarket US reached $10 billion for the month, exceeding its own prior monthly record by about 15%. Both figures were logged with a week still remaining in October.

Open interest followed volume higher. Kalshi's open interest reached $2.05 billion; Polymarket US posted $384 million. These aren't just trading flow numbers — sustained open interest indicates that participants are holding positions, not just flipping contracts for short-term noise. That's the behavioral signature of a market being used for actual exposure management, not only speculation.

The market structure shift is perhaps the starkest data point of all. A year ago, Kalshi's entire October volume was approximately $4.4 billion — roughly on par with Polymarket's international exchange at the time. This October, Kalshi generated 18.7 times Polymarket Global's volume. The US regulatory perimeter, anchored by CFTC oversight, has become the gravitational center of the industry: Kalshi and Polymarket US together accounted for 95.6% of combined October volume across the three tracked platforms.

"Kalshi now accounts for nearly 83% of total volume between itself, Polymarket US and Polymarket Global."

- The Block, Data & Insights, October 28, 2026

Polymarket's pivot toward its US operation followed its acquisition of CFTC-licensed derivatives exchange QCEX for $112 million — a strategic bet that CFTC regulation, not offshore permissiveness, is the sustainable growth model. That bet is now paying off in volume terms, even as Polymarket Global's share of activity shrinks.

Why the Volume Ratio Is the Metric to Watch

The Block's prediction-market-to-spot-exchange volume ratio hit 11.42 in October, up from 7.34 in September. That ratio has risen in ten of the last twelve months. To be precise about what this measures: it's a dataset-specific comparison, not a claim that prediction markets process more dollars than all crypto spot venues globally. But the directional signal is clear and consistent — prediction markets are capturing a growing share of event-driven financial activity relative to traditional crypto trading.

Robinhood's financials add commercial texture to that trend. Its prediction-market business generated $173 million in third-quarter revenue, compared with $120 million from cryptocurrency trading — and previously generated 21% more revenue from event contracts than equity trading in the second quarter. Sports remains its largest prediction category, but Bitcoin and gold contracts are identified as expanding areas. That a major retail brokerage now earns more from event contracts than from crypto spot is not a footnote; it's a structural shift in how retail financial products are being consumed.

Kalshi has been building the sports-audience pipeline deliberately. A sponsorship agreement with the US Open is one visible piece of a broader effort to make prediction markets a mainstream companion to live events — normalizing the habit of trading outcomes the way fantasy sports normalized statistical engagement with games.

The Counter-View: October Is a Peak, Not a Baseline

The honest complication here is timing. October 2026 delivered a near-perfect volume storm: the final weeks before the November 3 US midterm elections, ongoing sports seasons, and macro uncertainty all converging. The volume records are real, but the open question is whether they represent a durable new floor or a concentrated spike around once-a-cycle events.

Post-election data will be the first real test. Election markets tend to collapse in open interest once outcomes are resolved, and if sports contracts can't absorb the slack, platforms will face a period of elevated infrastructure costs against declining revenues. The Block framed it precisely: November's midterms will show "whether election markets can add a second growth lever on top of sports." If they do, Kalshi and Polymarket US have a two-engine growth model. If political volume evaporates post-election and sports alone can't replace it, October's records start to look more like a ceiling than a foundation.

There's also competitive pressure Kalshi is navigating on a separate front entirely. Utah's attorney general has filed 14 criminal charges — including 13 felonies — against KalshiEx and co-founders Tarek Mansour and Luana Lopes Lara over alleged illegal online gambling. The case remains an overhang on what is otherwise an extraordinary growth story, and its resolution matters for Kalshi's regulatory standing at the state level even as it operates within federal CFTC jurisdiction.

What Comes After the Record

The midterm elections on November 3 are the immediate catalyst to watch — not because the outcome is uncertain, but because how prediction market volumes behave in the days immediately after resolution will reveal the real shape of the user base. Sticky participants who stay to trade the next cycle of sports, macro, and Fed-related contracts are the ones that matter for long-term platform economics.

The emergence of aggregator services like WagerBeasts — which compares sportsbook odds against prediction market prices — illustrates that a broader ecosystem is forming around these venues. That kind of infrastructure doesn't develop unless market participants expect the underlying platforms to be around for years, not months. It's a quiet but meaningful indicator of institutional confidence in the category's staying power.

What It Means

  • Kalshi is the dominant US venue: At 83% of combined tracked volume and 18.7x Polymarket Global's October figures, it has effectively won the domestic market share battle — for now. Sustained dominance depends on what happens after elections resolve.
  • CFTC regulation is the moat: The 95.6% volume concentration in CFTC-regulated venues signals that regulatory clarity, not permissiveness, is the winning model. Offshore venues are losing relevance as the US market matures.
  • November 3 is the inflection point: Post-midterm open interest data will determine whether October's records represent a new baseline or an election-cycle anomaly. Watch whether Kalshi's open interest holds above $2 billion into December.
  • Robinhood's revenue mix is a leading indicator: Event contracts now outearning crypto and equity trading at a major retail broker this likely means prediction market integrations will accelerate across brokerage platforms, not just native venues.
