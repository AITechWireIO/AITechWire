Phantom Integrates Kalshi Prediction Markets for US Users

Phantom wallet rolled out Kalshi-powered markets including 15-min Bitcoin contracts, sports, and politics to all eligible US users on Oct 29. Expands on-chain access to regulated event trading.

AI TechWire · October 29, 2026 · 4 min read
Photo by Deng Xiang / Unsplash

Phantom Brings Kalshi's Regulated Markets Into Solana's Orbit — and the Timing Is Loaded

Phantom just handed 20 million crypto users a direct line to regulated US prediction markets, and the architecture it used to get there may matter as much as the product itself. This is not a gimmick integration — it is a structural bet that the next wave of on-chain volume runs through event contracts, not just token swaps.

What Happened

On Wednesday, Phantom announced it was opening Kalshi's prediction markets to all eligible US users inside its wallet app. The rollout builds on an initial expansion that began October 23, with access widening over the following weeks. At launch, users can trade on cryptocurrency price moves — including Bitcoin contracts that settle every 15 minutes — plus sports, politics, economics, and AI-adjacent events, all without leaving the Phantom interface.

The mechanics are clean. Kalshi, registered with the US Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC), handles customer diligence, account approval, trade execution, and market resolution. Phantom supplies the interface. Trades are funded through a user's Phantom Cash balance, held in the CASH stablecoin. Positions are represented as Solana-based outcome tokens, visible in wallet activity and on blockchain explorers. The fee structure is straightforward: a combined $0.02 per contract, split evenly between a $0.01 Phantom platform fee and a $0.01 Kalshi exchange fee, with network costs potentially applying on top depending on how a trade is funded.

Each market also includes a live chat, and the trade interface is designed to feel like a token swap — minimal friction between viewing a market and placing a position.

Source: Femi Awomosu, Head of Product at Phantom - "Crypto traders already think in short windows, like where Bitcoin will be in 15 minutes. When a market closes that fast, every extra screen costs you the price you saw." link

Why It Matters: The Regulatory Scaffolding Is the Story

The product is interesting. The regulatory path Phantom used to get there is more interesting.

In March, the CFTC's Market Participants Division issued Staff Letter 26-09, granting Phantom no-action relief from registering as an introducing broker. The conditions are specific: Phantom cannot hold custody of user assets, generate express buy or sell signals, or exercise discretion over order routing. It must onboard users directly with a registered exchange, and a written undertaking makes Phantom and each partner jointly and severally liable for violations. Phantom says it was the first passive software provider to receive this relief. In September, the CFTC extended the same framework to similarly situated software providers industry-wide.

That September expansion is the sleeper development here. What Phantom negotiated in March as a one-off arrangement became a sector-wide template in under six months. Any wallet or app that fits the passive-software-provider definition now has a legal lane into regulated prediction markets. Phantom was first, but it is unlikely to be last.

"Phantom did the regulatory work up front, and it's a model for how prediction markets can reach more people."

- Kevin Jacobs, General Counsel, Phantom

The Solana angle compounds the significance. Outcome tokens live on-chain, settlement flows back through Phantom Cash, and the entire experience is native to Solana's infrastructure. If volume builds, it builds on Solana — adding a new, regulated category of activity to a chain that has already absorbed significant retail trading flow.

The Counter-View: Shadow Over Kalshi's House

The launch does not exist in a vacuum. On October 28 — the day Phantom announced the wider rollout — Utah filed 14 felony counts against Kalshi CEO Tarek Mansour and COO Luana Lopes Lara, related to alleged unlawful sports betting promotion. The charges are state-level and the outcome remains uncertain, but the timing is striking: the exchange powering Phantom's new US feature is simultaneously facing criminal allegations from a state attorney general.

This is the tension Phantom has to manage. The CFTC's no-action relief gives Phantom federal regulatory cover, and Kalshi's CFTC registration is intact. But Kalshi's jurisdictional patchwork — state-level restrictions already limit which markets are available in which states — means the legal surface area is wide. Phantom's own disclosures direct users to review its US prediction market terms before participating, and access remains subject to identity verification, state restrictions, and eligibility requirements. The architecture was built for exactly this kind of complexity, but the Utah charges are a live variable that neither company controls.

There is also a liquidity question embedded in the product itself. Phantom's documentation notes that selling a position before market resolution depends on available liquidity — an open position may not always be closed immediately at the desired price. For 15-minute Bitcoin contracts, where price moves can be sharp and windows short, that caveat carries real weight for active traders.

Resolution: Who Wins, What to Watch

Phantom's user base gains access to a regulated, on-chain prediction market layer that did not exist for them two weeks ago. Kalshi gains distribution through a wallet claiming more than 20 million users — a significant reach multiplier for an exchange that has spent years fighting for regulatory legitimacy. Solana gains another category of on-chain economic activity, with settlement tokens and trade flows running through its infrastructure.

The open question is whether the Utah charges evolve into something that threatens Kalshi's operating status or remains a contained state-level dispute. Federal registration and state-level legal exposure can coexist for a long time, but they cannot coexist indefinitely without resolution. For Phantom, the bet is that the CFTC framework holds and the distribution advantage compounds. That bet looks reasonable today. Whether it still looks reasonable in six months depends heavily on how the Kalshi legal situation develops — and on whether competing wallets move quickly into the lane the CFTC opened in September.

What It Means

  • Phantom users gain a new asset class: Regulated event contracts — Bitcoin, sports, politics, economics — are now accessible within the same app where they already trade and hold crypto, with outcome tokens settling on Solana.
  • The CFTC's September industry-wide relief is the bigger story: Phantom pioneered the passive-software-provider framework, but its extension to all similarly situated providers likely means competing wallets will follow; Phantom's first-mover advantage is real but time-limited.
  • Kalshi's legal exposure is the primary risk to watch: The Utah felony charges against Kalshi's CEO and COO filed October 28 create uncertainty that Phantom's federal regulatory cover does not fully neutralize — state-level outcomes here could affect which markets remain available and where.
  • Solana is the infrastructure winner regardless: Every outcome token, every settlement, and every trade routed through Phantom runs on Solana — this likely means measurable new on-chain volume for the network as the user base engages with markets that close as fast as every 15 minutes.
