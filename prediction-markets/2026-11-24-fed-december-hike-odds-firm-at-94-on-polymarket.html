Fed December Hike Odds Firm at 94% on Polymarket

Polymarket prices a 25bp Fed rate hike in December at 94% with pause odds at just 4%, driven by dollar strength to 20-month highs and inflation concerns ahead of PCE data. AI stock rally has reinforced rate hike bets among traders.

AI TechWire · November 24, 2026 · 4 min read
Photo by Deng Xiang / Unsplash

Prediction markets have made their call: the Federal Reserve is hiking rates in December, and nearly every dollar on Polymarket agrees. With 94% odds locked onto a 25-basis-point increase at the December 8–9 FOMC meeting, traders are not debating the outcome — they are positioning for the fallout.

The Market Has Spoken

As of November 24, 2026, Polymarket's market on the Fed's December decision has seen $55.1 million traded across all outcomes. The 25-basis-point hike sits at 94%. A pause — no change — commands just 4%. A hike of 50 basis points or more draws 2.9%. The odds of any cut are essentially rounding errors: a 25-basis-point cut at 0.3%, a cut of 50 or more at 0.1%.

This was not always the consensus. On July 30, the probability of a December hike stood at just 42%. The climb to 94% unfolded in stages, punctuated by sharp single-day moves. On November 7, odds jumped 12 points in a single session — from 65% to 77%. Three days later they surged another 10 points. By November 16, they were at 92%. A brief four-point dip on November 21 barely registered before the market reasserted its conviction.

Over the past 30 days alone, the probability rose 17 percentage points. That is not noise — that is a market that has heard the evidence and reached a verdict.

"25 bps increase leads No change by 90 points. Flipping the order would take sustained news, not one headline."

— PredictRips, analyzing Polymarket data, November 24, 2026

What Is Driving the Conviction

The clearest near-term catalyst is data. The U.S. Personal Consumption Expenditures Price Index for October is scheduled for release on November 25. Analysts expect the print to show how sticky inflation remains — and sticky is the operative word. Under Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, who took office in May 2026, the FOMC has demonstrated a willingness to tighten even as markets wobble.

Source: BTSE COO Jeff Mei — "The PCE print may show how sticky inflation remains." link

What the prediction market is pricing is not just one data point. It reflects a broader macro shift: the Fed's December decision defines what dollars cost going into 2027. Higher rates raise the return on cash and bonds, lifting the threshold every risk asset must clear. That math is straightforward, and market participants have been acting on it for weeks.

The equity tape confirms the pressure. The Nasdaq Composite fell approximately 10% and the S&P 500 dropped 8.6% in the week ending November 20. Those are not minor corrections. They are the market repricing the cost of capital in real time.

Crypto Takes the Hit First

Bitcoin is the most visible casualty of the rate-hike repricing. The asset opened November at $77,582 on Binance, lost roughly 21% of its value over the month, and touched a low of approximately $55,130 on November 21. By November 23 it had recovered to near $61,000 — a stabilization, not a reversal.

The ETF flow data tells a sharper story. U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs recorded $4.14 billion in net outflows in November through November 20 — the second-largest monthly outflow of 2026 and within 8% of the all-time record of $4.51 billion set in June. BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust accounted for $1.37 billion of the $1.79 billion lost in the week of November 16–20 alone. On consecutive days — November 19 and 20 — IBIT set new daily outflow records of $541.1 million and $544.7 million respectively, bringing its two-day total to $1.09 billion.

Four consecutive weeks of outflows have drained a net $4.34 billion from spot bitcoin ETFs. Total net assets, which stood near $110 billion in mid-September, fell to $71.35 billion by November 20 — a decline of roughly 35% in about two months.

Source: Dan Khus, chief analyst at LVRG Research — "The four-week streak looks more structural than earlier episodes, coinciding with sustained price pressure, elevated liquidations and a shift toward capital preservation." link

The Bitcoin Fear and Greed Index read 11 — extreme fear — as of the week ending November 20. Polymarket's own Bitcoin price markets price just a 1.5% chance of bitcoin reaching $100,000 before year-end 2026.

The Counter-Case and Why It Is Losing

The bear case for a hike — that the Fed might pause to stabilize falling equity markets and crypto — is precisely what the 4% "no change" price represents. It exists. It is just being consistently sold.

Rick Maeda, Head of Markets at Laevitas, described the crypto outflows as mostly risk reduction, noting that profit-taking normally follows a rally. The problem: these withdrawals arrived in a falling market, with bitcoin sliding from around $80,000 at the start of November to the low $60,000s. That distinction matters. This is not post-rally rotation. It is capital preservation in advance of a policy tightening that the market sees as nearly certain.

Source: Zeus Research analyst Dominick John — "Bitcoin and ether ETFs may face further outflows if rate-hike concerns and weak risk appetite persist." link

U.S. spot ether ETFs extended their own losing streak to eight consecutive weeks of outflows, with $314 million leaving in the week of November 16–20. The category has now tied the longest losing streak on record. Rate sensitivity is not a Bitcoin-specific story.

What It Means

  • Crypto faces structural headwinds: With the December hike priced at 94% and PCE data due November 25, any upside surprise in inflation this likely means another leg of ETF outflows, potentially pushing November past June's all-time record of $4.51 billion.
  • IBIT is the pressure point: BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust accounted for roughly 76% of weekly spot bitcoin ETF outflows — its performance in the final November sessions is the number to watch for whether the monthly outflow record falls.
  • Growth stocks remain vulnerable: The Nasdaq's 10% weekly decline signals the rate-hike repricing is broader than crypto; if the December hike lands as priced, further compression in high-multiple equities is the logical follow-on.
  • The surprise risk cuts both ways: Prediction markets move most on the gap between expectation and outcome. At 94%, a hike is fully priced — the asymmetric risk is a surprise pause, which would likely spark a sharp relief rally across risk assets.
