Polymarket: Jensen Huang 87% odds to exit Nvidia CEO by YE

Polymarket contract on Huang departure by end-2026 surged to 87% from 16.5% prior day; Kalshi shows 69-71% by Nov 13. Shift signals investor skepticism on Nvidia leadership amid AI compute scrutiny.

AI TechWire · November 12, 2026 · 4 min read
Photo by Brecht Corbeel / Unsplash

The Market Has Spoken: Prediction Traders Are Betting Jensen Huang Is Gone by New Year's

When a prediction market moves from 16.5% to 87% in a single trading day, that is not noise — that is the crowd pricing in a near-certainty. On November 12, 2026, Polymarket's contract on Jensen Huang announcing his resignation or removal as Nvidia CEO by December 31 did exactly that, and the AI chip sector is now absorbing the implications of a potential leadership vacuum at the most consequential company in the artificial intelligence supply chain.

What Happened: A One-Day Repricing That Rewrote the Story

The numbers are stark. Polymarket's year-end departure contract surged from approximately 16.5% to roughly 87% on November 12, with the platform recording approximately $17.4 million in total trading volume on the broader market. The December 31, 2026 outcome alone attracted about $4.8 million in contract volume — a figure that reflects genuine capital at risk, not casual speculation. Meanwhile, Kalshi, the CFTC-regulated event-contract exchange, moved its own year-end market from a prior-day close of 17% to approximately 90%, with its contract pricing Huang's departure before January 1, 2027, at 86% in its November 11 update.

Both platforms also ran a near-term contract covering departure before November 13 Eastern Time. Polymarket put that at 69%; Kalshi, at 71%. The alignment between a crypto-native decentralized platform and a US-regulated exchange — two platforms with different user bases, settlement mechanics, and regulatory frameworks — is precisely what makes this price action impossible to dismiss.

The trigger was a twin news event on the evening of November 11. The New York Times reported a second alleged affair between Huang and a direct report. The Wall Street Journal, citing sources, reported that Nvidia's board was preparing to meet to consider removing him. Within hours, Polymarket's contract had already begun repricing. By November 12, both platforms had converged on odds that, in any rational probability framework, signal near-certain departure.

"Polymarket's market… had recorded approximately $17.4 million in total trading volume as of November 12. The December 31, 2026 outcome was priced at 82%, with about $4.8 million in volume attached to that specific contract."

- CryptoTimes, November 12, 2026

Why It Matters: Governance Risk Meets Geopolitical Exposure

The story driving the market repricing is not simply a corporate soap opera. It carries a national-security dimension that elevates the stakes considerably. Reports surfaced of a relationship between Jensen Huang and Alice Huang, a former executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed, whom US authorities reportedly suspected of links to Chinese intelligence. Those allegations have not been established in court, and Nvidia's board stressed on November 8 that it had no reason to believe US national security or Nvidia's trade secrets had been compromised. But the board announced an independent investigation the same day, overseen by a committee of independent directors with outside counsel — a formal process with formal consequences.

Huang himself sent a message to Nvidia employees on November 8 acknowledging infidelity, apologizing to his family and colleagues, and stating his continued commitment to the company. He did not identify the person involved. That statement, intended to manage the narrative, instead accelerated the board's deliberations and the market's repricing.

The governance mechanics are worth understanding. Nvidia's board bylaws permit a majority-vote removal of the CEO, notwithstanding the fact that Huang holds only 3.58% of shares. That structure means the board has clear procedural authority to act — and the market is betting heavily that it will. If Huang is removed, Reuters and Bloomingbit reporting identifies Ian Buck, Nvidia's vice president overseeing the data center business, as the leading internal successor candidate, with Buck also registering the highest odds of becoming next CEO on Polymarket.

The Counter-View: Settlement Rules Are Not the Same as Outcomes

The strongest pushback against the market's implied certainty sits inside the fine print of the contracts themselves — and it matters. Polymarket settles on a public announcement of resignation or removal, regardless of the actual departure date. Kalshi requires that Huang actually leave the CEO position, with that departure announced by him or an authorized representative. Critically, both platforms explicitly exclude suspension, administrative leave, temporary changes to duties, and indefinite leave from their settlement conditions.

This is not a minor technical distinction. If Nvidia's board chooses to place Huang on leave — administrative or otherwise — while the independent investigation proceeds, neither platform's contracts would resolve as "Yes." Traders long on departure would receive no payout, even if Huang is functionally sidelined for months. The board has an incentive to move carefully and the procedural tools to do so without triggering a formal removal announcement before year-end. The 87–90% odds on both platforms do not fully price that optionality in the board's favor.

The open question, then, is whether the board will move to a formal removal announcement on a timeline that satisfies the contracts — or whether it will manage the crisis through intermediate steps that leave the market in limbo through December 31.

Resolution: What the Trade Structure Tells You

When Polymarket prices a by-November-30 outcome at 80% and a by-December-31 outcome at 82%, the spread is narrow — implying traders believe any departure announcement, if it comes, comes soon rather than late in the year. The Kalshi ladder reinforces this: 80% before December 1, 86% before January 1, 90% before July 1, 2027. The market is not pricing a drawn-out succession process; it is pricing a fast-moving board decision.

What the two markets together are telling the AI chip sector is that the most recognizable face in AI infrastructure — the architect of Nvidia's GPU dominance, the co-founder who turned a graphics card company into the backbone of the global AI buildout — has an approximately one-in-eight chance of remaining in his current role through the end of this year. For an industry that has priced Nvidia's moat as partly a function of Huang's singular vision, that is a material risk factor, not a footnote.

What It Means

  • Board decision timeline is the critical variable: If Nvidia's board opts for administrative leave rather than a formal removal announcement, departure contracts on both Polymarket and Kalshi would not resolve as "Yes" — this likely means traders holding long positions face meaningful settlement risk even if the odds hold near 87–90%.
  • Ian Buck becomes a name to watch: Polymarket traders are already pricing Buck, Nvidia's data center VP, as the leading internal successor; his elevation would signal continuity in Nvidia's core AI infrastructure business, which is the market's primary concern.
  • AI chip sector sentiment hinges on succession clarity: The longer the board deliberation extends without a formal announcement, the more uncertainty compresses into Nvidia's stock and volatility markets — investors should treat the prediction market ladder as a real-time sentiment gauge, not a verdict.
  • The national-security dimension is not priced away: The independent board investigation into the foreign-intelligence reporting remains active; its findings, independent of any CEO decision, could carry regulatory and trade implications for Nvidia's business in ways that outlast the leadership question itself.
