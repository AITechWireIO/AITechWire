Prediction Markets See Record Volumes on Fed/AI

Kalshi and Polymarket traded heavily on recent Fed decision and AI-related contracts. Growing liquidity underscores integration of events, crypto rails, and AI pricing.

AI TechWire · September 18, 2026 · 5 min read
Photo by Cht Gsml / Unsplash

Prediction markets don't lie — and right now, they're screaming. As the Federal Reserve delivered its first rate hike in more than three years and the CLARITY Act collapsed in the Senate, Kalshi and Polymarket absorbed record contract volumes, turning macro chaos into tradable signal. The confluence of a hawkish Fed, a stalled crypto bill, and a crypto market that stubbornly refused to break is exactly the kind of environment where event-driven trading thrives — and the numbers are proving it.

The Fed Pulls the Trigger, Markets Respond

On September 16, the Federal Open Market Committee, chaired by Kevin M. Warsh, delivered a unanimous 25 basis point rate hike, lifting the federal funds target to 3.75%–4%. It was the Fed's first increase in more than three years, and officials signaled at least one more hike remains on the table this year — with inflation now not expected to return to target until 2029. The news landed like a thunderclap across crypto and traditional markets alike.

Bitcoin briefly dipped, touching as low as $74,887 mid-week before clawing back toward $78,000 by Friday. Layer-2 and DeFi tokens led the recovery, with Starknet surging 18%, Arbitrum gaining 17%, and Uniswap jumping 13%. The DeFi Select Index accelerated 8.3% since midnight UTC Thursday and 16% over the prior 24 hours. The 10-year Treasury yield, which had been stoking inflation fears, slipped back below 5%, while Brent crude retreated below $103 after trading as high as $109 earlier in the week — giving crypto bulls the macro cover they needed.

For prediction market platforms, the Fed decision represented exactly the kind of high-stakes, binary-outcome event that drives liquidity. Contracts tied to rate decisions, inflation prints, and central bank posture are increasingly core offerings on both Kalshi — led by co-founder and CEO Tarek Mansour — and Polymarket, where founder and CEO Shayne Coplan has been aggressively expanding DeFi product integrations.

CLARITY Act Failure Opens a New Prediction Frontier

If the Fed hike was one engine driving prediction market volume, the CLARITY Act's Senate stumble was the other. On Tuesday, the bill — championed by Senator Cynthia Lummis of Wyoming, Chair of the Senate Banking Digital Assets Subcommittee — failed to reach the 60-vote threshold needed to advance, attracting just 49 supporters. Bitcoin briefly fell below $74,887 on the news before stabilizing, a relatively muted reaction that analysts interpreted as a sign traders had already priced in the legislative risk.

"What stands out to me is that Bitcoin has hardly budged at all in response to two objectively bad pieces of news. A 25-basis-point hike and the CLARITY Act failing to pass are both headlines that, in a different market environment, would have sent price meaningfully lower. Instead, we got basically nothing."

- Mitchell Askew, Head of Blockware Intelligence at Blockware

For prediction markets, the CLARITY Act saga is far from over — and that's the point. With the bill stalled and regulatory oversight of platforms like Kalshi and Polymarket remaining under CFTC Chairman Michael S. Selig's purview, traders have every incentive to hedge legislative outcomes through event contracts. Each Senate procedural vote, each markup session, each floor debate becomes a fresh contract opportunity. The 49-50 cloture failure wasn't a conclusion — it was an invitation.

Crypto Rails, AI Pricing, and the Liquidity Flywheel

Beyond the immediate headlines, this week's volume surge underscores a structural shift in how event risk is being priced. The crypto futures market signaled a revival in positional trading, with cumulative open interest expanding nearly 5% to $141.2 billion — even as daily trading volume dipped 3% to $95 billion. That divergence — more open interest, less spot churn — suggests capital is entering the market structurally, not through aggressive momentum chasing.

Uniswap futures open interest surged to 86.61 million tokens, flirting with an all-time high and up sharply from 76.89 million the prior day, coinciding with a 30% explosion in the token's spot price. Analysts at CoinDesk attributed the renewed DeFi appetite to mounting optimism around coordinated, friendly crypto regulation from both the SEC and CFTC — a forward-looking bet on regulatory clarity even as the CLARITY Act itself sits in legislative limbo.

Global crypto market capitalization rose 2.16% to $2.67 trillion on Friday, according to CoinMarketCap. The Fear & Greed Index held at 65, firmly in "greed" territory. Yet beneath the surface, caution lingers: spot Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs saw roughly $592 million in combined outflows earlier in the week, and Bitcoin and Ethereum funds posted $1.11 billion in combined outflows over two days.

Source: Riya Sehgal, Research Analyst, Delta Exchange - "The resilience is notable given that spot Bitcoin and Ethereum ETFs saw roughly $592 million in combined outflows earlier this week... for now, the market appears to be consolidating rather than reversing decisively." link

The Verdict: Event Risk Is Now a Portfolio Asset Class

This week crystallized something the prediction market industry has been building toward for years: event-driven contracts are no longer a sideshow. When the Fed hiked, when the CLARITY Act failed, when crude spiked above $109 and the Dollar Index topped 100 — every one of those moments was a tradable contract on Kalshi or Polymarket. The growing liquidity on these platforms, riding crypto rails and increasingly incorporating AI-driven pricing, means that portfolio managers ignoring prediction markets are leaving a hedge on the table.

Bitcoin is down just 1.5% in September — historically its worst month — and is still up roughly 32% for the quarter, on track for its first positive quarterly close since Q3 2025. As Mitchell Askew put it, when bad news stops moving the price, seller exhaustion has set in. That's a bottoming signal. And bottoming signals, like rate decisions and Senate votes, are exactly what prediction markets were built to trade.

The integration of macro events, crypto infrastructure, and AI-powered contract pricing isn't a future state. It's this week's volume. Finance hasn't caught up yet — but it will.
