Fed Hike Odds Rise; 4 Rate Hikes Priced by Mid-2027

Markets fully price Dec hike; persistent price pressures keep tightening in play.

AI TechWire · November 18, 2026 · 4 min read
Photo by Bryan Dickerson / Unsplash

The Federal Reserve's rate-hiking cycle is back, and markets are no longer debating whether — only how far. With a December hike fully priced and the probability of at least four quarter-point increases by mid-September 2027 now sitting above 50%, Wall Street is staring down a tightening regime that could unwind much of the AI-fueled bull market that defined this record-breaking year.

The Pricing Has Shifted — Decisively

On October 14, CME Group's FedWatch Tool — which uses 30-day Fed Funds futures prices to estimate the probability of changes to the federal funds target rate — put the odds of at least 100 basis points in rate hikes through the September 15, 2027 FOMC meeting at 42%. By November 14, that figure had climbed to 53%. That is not a drift. That is a conviction trade hardening in real time.

What moved the needle was a one-two punch of retail and inflation data. Better-than-expected retail sales on Tuesday followed hotter-than-expected inflation data the week before. Together, they left markets fully pricing a 25-basis-point hike in December, with a 14% probability of a 50-basis-point move on the table, according to CME FedWatch.

The dollar has responded accordingly, rising 0.18% to 103.8 on the dollar index — a 19-month high and a fresh April 2025 level. The euro briefly touched an April 2025 low. Sterling dropped 0.3% to a one-month low. The yen sits near 159.3 per dollar, uncomfortably close to the 160 threshold at which Japanese authorities intervened in July. Currency markets are treating this as a structural repricing, not a blip.

"To push the dollar meaningfully higher from here, we'll probably need to see markets pricing in a more aggressive rate path into 2027."

- Dilin Wu, Research Strategist, Pepperstone

Three Engines Driving Inflation — and the Fed's Resolve

CME FedWatch's forecast is not being driven by a single data point. According to Motley Fool's analysis of the tool, three distinct inflationary forces are keeping the pressure on.

First, economists at the New York Federal Reserve writing for Liberty Street Economics have flagged that President Trump's tariffs are boosting goods price inflation. Second, energy supply disruptions tied to the closure of the Strait of Hormuz continue to push fuel prices higher. Third, the AI infrastructure buildout has handed significant pricing power to AI hardware companies — a dynamic that feeds directly into capital expenditure cycles and corporate cost structures across the economy.

These are not transient shocks. Each has a structural component that could keep the Fed on offense well into 2027.

Fed Chair Kevin Warsh, whose four-year term began on May 22, has been explicit about his priorities. He has repeatedly stated the Fed will "deliver price stability" and return annualized long-term inflation — as measured by the Personal Consumption Expenditures Price Index — to 2%. He has also shelved forward-looking guidance, forcing markets to read economic data and policymaker commentary as the primary signal for the Fed's next move.

That posture had a direct market impact on Wednesday. New York Fed President John Williams said he favored tighter monetary policy, though he pushed back on the need for a more aggressive pace of action. The minutes from the Fed's most recent meeting — where it held rates unchanged — are due for release Wednesday and will be parsed closely by traders for any hint of internal debate about the pace of hikes ahead. Williams, alongside Governor Michael Barr and Minneapolis Fed President Neel Kashkari, was scheduled to speak later in the day.

The Counter-Case: Steam Running Out

Not every analyst reads this as a runaway tightening story. Pepperstone's Dilin Wu made the case that the dollar's rally may already be approaching its ceiling. With a December hike fully baked into pricing, further dollar appreciation requires markets to price an even more aggressive path into 2027 — and that is a higher bar. Wu's point is a useful one: front-loaded expectations often leave assets vulnerable to disappointment if the Fed pauses or softens its tone.

Williams' own remarks land in the same zone — hawkish on direction, but deliberately measured on pace. The open question is whether the trio of inflationary catalysts (tariffs, energy, AI hardware) proves sticky enough to force the Fed's hand on a 50-basis-point move or an accelerated sequence of hikes, or whether conditions stabilize enough to slow the cadence after December.

Traders currently see the benchmark fed rate rising by around 75 basis points 12 months from now, to a range of 4.50%–4.75%, per CME FedWatch. That's a more moderate path than the full 100-plus basis points implied by the 53% scenario — which means the market itself is split on whether the hawkish case fully plays out.

What Higher-for-Longer Means for the Bull Market

The stakes for equities are significant. This has been a record year — the Dow, S&P 500, and Nasdaq all reached new highs. But the market's valuation has been built on an AI-driven growth narrative that depends heavily on cheap capital and long-duration risk appetite. Four rate hikes over seven FOMC meetings would stress both. Growth stocks, which are most sensitive to discount rate changes, face the clearest headwind. A federal funds rate approaching 5% would reprice expected earnings aggressively.

The bond market would absorb pain alongside equities, with yields repricing higher across the curve. The currency dynamic adds a second layer of pressure for multinationals reporting in dollars. The year that began with record highs may close with a very different question dominating the market conversation: how much of the rally can survive a Fed that is no longer a tailwind.

What It Means

  • A December 25-basis-point hike is effectively locked in; the live debate is now whether a 50-basis-point move or an accelerated 2027 sequence forces a more aggressive repricing of risk assets.
  • Growth stocks and long-duration bonds face the most direct pressure if the federal funds rate reaches 4.75%–5.00% by mid-September 2027 — this likely means valuation compression even if earnings hold up.
  • Watch the Wednesday FOMC minutes and remarks from Williams, Barr, and Kashkari for any signal on pace; a more aggressive tone could break the dollar above levels that trigger yen intervention risk near 160.
  • The dollar's near-term ceiling may already be close — further gains require markets to price an even steeper 2027 rate path than currently implied, which is the key threshold to monitor heading into year-end.
