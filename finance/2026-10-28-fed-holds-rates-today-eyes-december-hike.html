Fed Holds Rates Today, Eyes December Hike

Impacts markets, crypto, and borrowing costs globally today.

AI TechWire · October 28, 2026 · 4 min read
Photo by Kelly Sikkema / Unsplash

Fed Holds, But December Looms: How One Rate Decision Is Dictating Everything from Equities to Bitcoin

The Federal Reserve held rates steady today, and the market barely blinked — because the real decision is already being priced in for December. This is not a pause. It is a setup.

With the Fed's benchmark rate sitting in a range of 3.75% to 4% following September's quarter-point hike — the first increase in roughly three years — Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh now faces a room full of investors who have already made up their minds about what comes next. CME FedWatch data puts the probability of a December hike above 80%. The question is not whether Warsh will move again. It is whether anything he says today changes the trajectory.

What Happened: A Hold That Isn't Really a Hold

Going into today's FOMC meeting, markets assigned less than a 10% probability to a rate increase, according to the CME Group's FedWatch tool. That made the decision to hold largely a foregone conclusion. But foregone does not mean consequence-free.

The awkward math is this: the Fed raised rates in September after core PCE — its preferred inflation gauge — posted a 0.2% monthly increase for August. Now, with September's core PCE reading on track to come in at 0.3%, the committee is sitting on its hands. That inconsistency is not lost on the market.

Source: Michael Feroli, Chief U.S. Economist, JPMorgan Chase — "Chair Warsh will have to explain why the committee hiked after a two-tenths core PCE print in September but is now holding after an expected three-tenths outturn. Disallowing follow-up questions won't resolve the challenge." link

Feroli's point is sharp: an unexplained hold less than a week before midterm elections — with inflation actually accelerating — hands critics the narrative that monetary policy is drifting toward political accommodation. Fed officials insist otherwise, and some regional presidents are not going quietly. Cleveland's Beth Hammack, Dallas's Lorie Logan, and Minneapolis's Neel Kashkari are all potential dissenting votes, each having voted against a hold as recently as July.

There is no dot plot update and no Summary of Economic Projections at this meeting, which strips out the usual scaffolding investors use to read the room. That makes Warsh's press conference the only real signal on offer today.

Why It Matters: Liquidity Is the Variable, and the Fed Controls It

Zoom out from the rate table and the stakes become clearer. The September hike unwound a significant portion of the easing cycle that drove risk assets — equities, crypto, and duration-sensitive instruments — to their 2025 highs. Inflation re-accelerated to 3.7% in September, its highest since May, led by energy and gasoline. UBS has extended its tightening forecast into March 2027. Every one of those data points tightens the screws.

Bitcoin is perhaps the cleanest expression of this dynamic. It trades near $81,400 as of this morning, roughly a third below the approximately $126,000 record it printed in October 2025. The cryptocurrency pays no yield, which means it effectively carries the longest duration in finance: its price is a pure bet on the future value and abundance of dollars. When the Fed floods the system, Bitcoin swells first. When the Fed reverses, Bitcoin sells first.

"Bitcoin is dealing with the uncertainty around equities and oil, alongside the cost of dollar funding. This week is about how firms plan around those conditions."

- Edgars Laimite, Co-founder, Gravity Team

Institutional commitment to bitcoin funding — from pension funds and banks that have steadily built exposure — will be shaped by whatever guidance Warsh delivers on December's path, Laimite said. Hawkish signals reinforce expectations of higher yields and a stronger dollar, both of which are headwinds for a yieldless asset. Softer guidance runs the opposite direction.

The equity market is threading the same needle. A solid GDP reading and strong tech earnings have provided a foundation for a modest risk recovery, with the Nasdaq catching a lift earlier this week. Bitcoin eventually followed equities higher, briefly trading above $80,500 before settling into a rangebound pattern that has persisted for days.

The Counter-View: The Selling May Already Be Done

Here is the strongest case for the bulls, and it deserves a fair hearing. Bitcoin actually rallied after the September hike — climbing from around $75,000 to $81,000 — because the hawkish pivot had already been priced in before the decision landed. Markets that price in bad news and then hold are not broken. They are processed.

The $80,000 level is not arbitrary support. Analysts estimate that the average cost basis for a large cohort of ETF investors and bitcoin-treasury companies sits right at $80,000 to $81,500. Roughly $57 billion has flowed into U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs in aggregate. That is a substantial pool of holders who are, right now, approximately at breakeven — and whose collective decision to hold or sell defines the floor.

The cracks, however, are visible. ETFs recorded combined outflows of roughly $773.5 million on October 22–23. Cathie Wood trimmed her bitcoin ETF position this week. Strategy paused a five-week buying streak. Miner MARA has been selling its holdings. The line is only as strong as that cohort's nerve, and the cohort is showing strain. Nearly $11 billion in bitcoin options are approaching expiry, adding a derivatives dimension to an already crowded week of catalysts.

With the PCE index — the data point that triggered September's hike — due Thursday, investors will have one more reading before December's meeting to recalibrate. That number may matter more than anything Warsh says today.

What It Means

  • December is the real meeting: Futures markets price better than 80% odds of a hike in December; today's hold shifts all attention to Warsh's tone and Thursday's PCE print as the deciding inputs.
  • Bitcoin's $80,000 floor is a stress test, not a guarantee: With ETF outflows accelerating and institutional buyers at their cost basis, hawkish Fed signals this likely means another leg lower for crypto; softer guidance could trigger the breakout traders are watching for.
  • Dissents to watch: If Hammack, Logan, or Kashkari vote against the hold, the signal is that the committee's center of gravity is already leaning toward December action — a meaningful read-through for bond and equity markets heading into year-end.
  • The political optics problem is real: An unexplained hold with core PCE rising and an election days away hands critics a ready-made narrative; Warsh's press conference must thread a narrow needle between credibility and caution.
