Fed Rate Hike Talk Weighs on Markets & Crypto

Renewed 0.5% hike expectations and strong dollar contributed to risk-off across equities and digital assets. Bitcoin ETF outflows accelerated as macro uncertainty grew.

AI TechWire · November 22, 2026 · 4 min read
Photo by Jakub Żerdzicki / Unsplash

The Crypto Crowd Is Leaving the Building — and the Fed Is Holding the Door Open

The great crypto unwind of late 2026 is not just a price story — it is a story about where speculative capital goes when the macro tide turns. With renewed Federal Reserve rate hike talk pressuring risk assets and a stronger dollar tightening financial conditions, bitcoin and ether are not simply falling. They are being abandoned, methodically, by the same investors who once swore by them.

What Happened: A Market in Freefall Across the Board

Bitcoin dropped below $56,000 on November 21, dragged lower alongside tech stocks and gold, with altcoins following it down in lockstep. Ether's situation is starker still: the second-largest cryptocurrency by market capitalization has shed nearly 60% of its value since October, falling below $1,600 — a level that matters because it marks a threshold for companies that hold the token as a treasury asset. Total crypto market capitalization has fallen to roughly $2 trillion, about half of last year's peak, according to Bloomberg reporting cited across multiple sources. Trading volumes keep shrinking. Capital is retreating from stablecoins, altcoins, and virtually every segment of the digital asset market.

The drivers are interconnected. Renewed talks about a possible 0.5% rate hike by the Federal Reserve — now led by Chair Kevin Warsh, who took the role in May 2026 — have rattled rate-sensitive assets across the board. The AI trade, which had served as a parallel speculative engine, is itself unraveling. The Nvidia leadership crisis is still in full effect. And fresh liquidity is migrating toward newly available vehicles, including the recently launched Anthropic IPO. This is not one shock. It is a convergence of pressures arriving simultaneously, and crypto is absorbing all of them at once.

"Crypto is leaving the zeitgeist."

— James Steinberg, New York organizer

Where the Money Is Going: AI, Prediction Markets, and Cash

Speculative appetite has not vanished. It has redirected. According to a Bloomberg report, once-loyal bitcoin investors are rotating into AI-linked stocks, placing bets on prediction market platforms such as Kalshi, and trading commodity prices through perpetual futures contracts. Kalshi is reportedly taking in millions since it added commodity perpetuals. Former digital asset traders who used to buy every dip are now accumulating cash, waiting for what they believe will be better entry points elsewhere.

The anecdote that captures this shift most cleanly comes from Scott Kantrowitz, a 56-year-old retail investor from New York, who liquidated his bitcoin and ether ETF holdings and moved the proceeds into SpaceX and Anthropic shares. His explanation was blunt: "I got bored of it." That sentiment — boredom, not fear — is worth pausing on. Fear implies a temporary exit with intent to return. Boredom implies something closer to a permanent reallocation of attention and capital.

James Steinberg, who organized a gathering in New York satirically titled "rehome a crypto bro," put it even more directly: "Crypto is leaving the zeitgeist." The gathering itself — pitching former crypto investors on activities ranging from coin collecting to doomscrolling, explicitly excluding crypto content — captures the cultural shift as much as any price chart.

The Counter-View: Industry Voices Push Back

Not everyone inside the industry accepts the narrative of terminal decline. Bitwise CEO Matt Hougan, commenting on social media, stressed that the crypto industry should strive to improve the U.S. financial system — or risk losing the battle for public mindshare permanently. His framing is notable: he is not arguing that prices will recover on their own, but that the industry needs to earn its relevance by delivering genuine utility. That is a meaningfully different argument from the reflexive "zoom out" defenses of previous cycles.

The honest tension here is that Hougan's prescription — improve the system, demonstrate value — takes time crypto may not have if the macro environment stays hostile. Chair Warsh's Fed has signaled a posture that prioritizes inflation control, and a stronger dollar compounds pressure on every dollar-denominated risk asset. Bitcoin is facing what Bloomberg describes as its longest weekly losing streak since 2022. The dip-buyers have not disappeared; they have simply converted to cash-holders, waiting for a macro catalyst that is not yet visible on the horizon.

Resolution: Defensive Positioning Is Not Cowardice, It's the Trade

The macro logic here is straightforward. When the central bank signals tighter policy, the discount rate on future cash flows rises, compressing valuations on growth assets — including crypto, which offers no yield and prices in almost entirely on sentiment and future adoption curves. Meanwhile, higher yields become an investable asset in themselves. The capital rotation into AI stocks, prediction markets, and cash is rational behavior in this environment, not panic. It is the market repricing speculative assets against a backdrop of real return alternatives.

What the crypto market is experiencing right now is not unique in the history of speculative cycles. The open question is whether this correction resets the asset class for a new generation of entrants — or whether the cultural moment, as Steinberg suggests, has genuinely passed. Bitcoin's performance over the coming weeks relative to the Fed's next policy signals will be the clearest read on that question.

What It Means

  • Rate hike expectations are the dominant macro pressure on crypto right now; any softening in Fed language under Chair Warsh would likely be the single biggest near-term relief valve for digital assets.
  • Ether's drop below $1,600 creates direct balance-sheet risk for companies that hold it as a treasury asset — this likely means forced selling pressure could compound market weakness further.
  • Prediction market platforms like Kalshi and AI-linked equities are the near-term beneficiaries of crypto's capital flight; watch volume and inflow data on both for confirmation.
  • Bitcoin's longest weekly losing streak since 2022 signals that the "buy the dip" reflex has broken — until macro conditions shift, cash and yield-bearing instruments likely outperform speculative digital assets.
